Problem page: where every piece of information goes

Nothing on today’s page is dropped. The page keeps one order — the problem, its statements, how to settle it, its review, its people, what is known, the record — and everything about one statement lives in the selected-statement panel, under five tabs.

AThe selected-statement panel, tab by tab

Today the panel is one long column with folds inside folds, and some of a statement’s record (depends on, witness, findings) is only on its own page. Tabs make each kind of information one click away for any statement you select, and “Open its page” stays for the full record.

Claim

  • The claim in mathematics (KaTeX)
  • What the Lean means, where it differs (tsum, n = 0 …)
  • Words: unverified / approved, history, Rename or correct
  • The Lean (folded)
  • Why it ends in sorry (folded)

Proof

  • Direct proof, or the routes that close it
  • Each split: the partial proof, merged in, attestation
  • Its holes, each with status and claim
  • Read the partial proof (folded)
  • Closing it through its holes
  • Outlines

Today the longest part of the panel; on erdos-257 it lists 2 splits and 18 holes.

Known

  • Literature status: open / known / elementary, who proposed it, confirmed or awaiting
  • References and summary
  • Notes, with full text links
  • Confirm or dispute (steward)

Work

  • Plans
  • Approaches recorded as tried
  • Attempts, failed ones included, with where they stopped
  • Pull requests in flight
  • How to contribute

Record

  • Hash, origin, Mathlib pin
  • Files: Statement.lean, attestation, Proof.lean
  • Depends on, used by
  • Witness, acknowledged findings
  • Glosses and explainers, and their approval state
  • How to read this panel
