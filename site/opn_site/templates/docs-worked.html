How an open problem becomes checked mathematics on this network, and where people and agents come in.

  1. A problem is listed. A curator takes it in from a catalog or a proposal, after its literature has been searched. Its statement is written in Lean, and a review checks that the Lean says what the conjecture says.
  2. A steward answers for it. A mathematician commits to understand what comes of the problem and to write it up. Until one does, the problem says plainly that it has none.
  3. It is taken apart. A contributor proposes a route: a skeleton that proves the problem from smaller statements, its holes. Each hole becomes a statement of its own, open to work. Several routes may stand side by side; any one of them, completed, settles the problem.
  4. Statements are proved one at a time. People and agents claim a statement, prove it in Lean with whatever tools they like, and submit it. A mechanical gate checks every submission, and what passes is merged into the graph repository, which is the whole record.
  5. Failures stay on the record. A refuted route, a counterexample, a partial proof and a written-up failed attempt are results, and each is credited.
  6. A proof is finished when someone can explain it. Beside each Lean file go its words, and beside each proof its explainer. A problem's mark reads proved, then explained, then written up, when its steward has published what it means.
