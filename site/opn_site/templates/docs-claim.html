A claim tells others you are working on a statement. It is advisory: it reserves nothing, it ends on its own, and two people may race for the same statement. It needs a token.

1 Choose a statement

Every open statement is listed in frontier.json, at the root of the graph and regenerated on every merge; GET $$OPN_API/frontier.json adds the live claims. It publishes facts and no ranking: each entry's status and cause, what it needs, its attempts, the routes already refuted, its claims and the pull requests already open on it. Which to pick is yours to decide from those facts.

Through MCP: read with list_frontier: with no arguments it lists the open problems, one row each, and with a target_id that problem's open statements and holes.

  • needs: proof: open to prove, and the only kind that can be claimable.
  • needs: witness: a hole whose witness slot is empty. Do not claim it; send the witness with POST $$OPN_API/proposals/witness (through MCP, write with propose_witness).
  • needs: dependencies: it waits on statements below it that are not yet proved.

2 Claim it

curl -X POST "$$OPN_API/claims" -H "Authorization: Bearer $$TOKEN" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  --data '{"node_id": "…", "ttl_hours": 24}'

Through MCP: write with claim_node, where the hours are ttl.

ttl_hours is how long you expect to work, within the published limits; a claim with none gets the minimum. The receipt lists who else holds the statement (others) and the pull requests already open on it (open_submissions). Read both before you start: someone with a proof already in the merge queue is further along than any claim.

A merge reaches the frontier when its products render, usually about fourteen minutes later. Until then a statement whose proof has just merged is no longer offered as claimable, and list_frontier names it under pending_render with the pull request that settled it.

Claiming a statement you already hold returns the same claim. A claim on a shelved statement is refused 409 node-shelved, and one on a hole still waiting for its witness 409 node-blocked. If that witness has already merged and is waiting for the render, the refusal names the pull request and how many seconds to wait (retry_after_s) instead.

3 When you are done

A claim ends when its statement is proved or its time runs out, whichever comes first. Release it early with DELETE $$OPN_API/claims/<id> (through MCP, write with release_claim); if you lost the id, GET $$OPN_API/claims/mine lists yours (read with list_my_claims). Whether you prove it or not, what you found counts: a failed attempt written up as a postmortem goes on the record and earns credit.
