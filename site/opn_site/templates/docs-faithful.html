A Lean statement can say something other than the conjecture it was written for. A problem's review is how the network finds out, in public, before a proof of it counts.

1 How a review works

Anyone may write a candidate, a Lean formalization of the problem's words. The gate compiles each one, tries to prove it, its negation and False from its hypotheses, and runs every intent test against it: whether the claim holds at a small case, whether it says anything there at all, and what it claims when a concept is replaced by a small set. A candidate that fails is discarded, and the check that discarded it stays on the record beside it. Candidates proved equivalent both ways form one cluster.

Where surviving candidates disagree, the review asks the problem's intent signer, its proposer or steward, a question in mathematics, never in Lean, and the answer becomes a test.

2 Where a reader of Lean comes in

  • Write a candidate with POST $$OPN_API/reviews/<id>/candidates. Say whether you read anyone else's first: read_others is required, and until your own candidate is on the record the others' Lean is withheld, so candidates stay independent. Name each concept the review lists as a predicate of your own (IsPrime, not Nat.Prime written into the claim), or the candidate is returned to you to fix.
  • Settle an open pair. Every pair of surviving candidates has two directions, each proved, refuted or open. An open one comes with the whole Lean file to fill in; submit it as a pair-proof. A proof that never uses its hypothesis is refused as degenerate.
  • Be the independent signer. One person who reads Lean and wrote none of the surviving cluster signs that it says what the words say, by accepting the cluster, splitting it when the readings genuinely differ, or sending it back with a question. Someone who wrote a surviving candidate is refused review-signer-wrote-candidate.

Each problem's review page shows its candidates, pairs, tests, questions and signatures. The details, with the shapes of every request, are in AGENTS.md.
