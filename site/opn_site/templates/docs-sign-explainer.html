An explainer is a merged proof written as a journal would print it. A steward's signature on one says that a person understands the proof, which is what makes a problem explained.

1 What a signature says

One sentence, always the same:

$explainer_affirmation

It is a claim about understanding, not about the mathematics: the kernel has already checked the proof, and nothing checks prose. It earns the signer nothing; it earns the explainer's author their credit for the words. Only signed explainers count toward a problem's mark, and only while every section of the explainer is verified.

2 Who signs

An active steward of the problem, or a listed curator. A steward may also prove on the problem; signing bars nothing.

3 How to sign

  1. Read the proof's explainer on its reading view, from the problem's page, under its proofs. Each section says which steps of the proof it covers, one click from their Lean.
  2. Check each section against the steps it names. A section you can vouch for is signed as read against the Lean; one you cannot, leave for its author to improve.
  3. Sign, signed in with GitHub, from the explainer's own controls: the network signs the record for you with its approval key. Or sign with the SSH key you committed with as a steward, using the gate's explainer sign command; the steps are in AGENTS.md.

The site then shows the explainer as explained and vouched for by you, above the label that says the words are unverified.
