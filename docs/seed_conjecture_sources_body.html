There is one real source. Google DeepMind's formal-conjectures registry holds essentially every reviewed Lean statement of an open problem. Everything else either vendors its statements from there or is unreviewed AI output.

Its checking is good enough to inherit from, not good enough to skip review. Every statement gets human review, but by one approver, with no per-statement record and no "verified" tier. The registry has itself corrected 291 misformalizations, about 11% of its statements, most of them found by provers rather than reviewers.

A filtered subset gets you what you actually want. Statements that Thomas Bloom selected for FrontierMath Erdős, that survived AlphaProof Nexus and Aletheia's prover and literature sweeps, and that use only Mathlib definitions, carry enough external evidence to meet D-9's top grade on its operational definition. The remaining cost per target is one non-author back-translation, which you can do yourself with tooling in well under an hour. The catalog below applies that filter to every formalized Erdős problem.

1. The grading scale

D-9 makes fidelity a graded status, not a yes/no. The grades, low to high, with the floor below which no proving compute is allocated:

mechanical-onlyback-translatedauthor-attestedexpert-attestedpublished-and-uncontested

The top grade is operational, not reputational: N days public in a D-10 venue, M independent proof attempts, zero confirmed defect claims. Pilot defaults are N = 30 and M = 3. That definition is the lever in this report, because the registry's public history already supplies both N and M for many statements.

3. How Formal Conjectures actually checks a statement

Verified from the repository's CONTRIBUTING.md, AGENTS.md, CI workflows, review records and the May 2026 paper (arXiv 2605.13171).

Intake

Anyone with a Google CLA submits. Every statement carries exactly one @[category research open | research solved | textbook | API | test], at least one @[AMS n], and a module docstring citing the source. Questions are written with answer(sorry). AGENTS.md adds a Source Fidelity rule: match quantifier order, bounds and hypotheses; test edge cases.

Mechanical checks

lake --wfail build and test, a category-contradiction script, copyright headers, and custom linters: ExistsImplicationLinter (catches the classic ∃ x, P x → Q bug), StubLinter (no sorry in definitions, no new axioms), metadata linters. There is no triviality or vacuity check in CI.

Human review

The paper says every contribution gets review by someone with Lean and domain expertise. In practice the two most recent merged PRs each had one approval, by the lead maintainer. No CODEOWNERS file, no per-statement reviewer record, no "verified" label. Fixed statements do not retain a record of the old, wrong version.

Adversarial provers

The strongest check, and it runs outside CI: AlphaProof, Gemini and Aristotle are pointed at the registry, and "manually inspecting these results primarily reveals misformalizations." Nearly every incident in §5 was caught this way.

Link to erdosproblems.com

Mechanical in both directions. A push to the registry auto-updates the site's "Formalised statement? Yes" link, which points at main, not a commit. The site records no formalization-verified status and Bloom does not review the Lean, except the 18 Epoch statements.

Measured defect rate

291 misformalizations corrected up to the frozen bench-v1 tag of 2,615 statements. By class: misrepresentation (quantifiers, connectives, missing hypotheses) 48%, semantic (types, operators) 35%, implicit conventions 8%, reporting 5%, syntactic 3%, genuine mathematical error 0.3%.

5. What review misses on open problems

Concrete cases, all from the Erdős subset, grouped by the defect class. Each of these is a wording pattern the catalog screens for.

  • Source ambiguity. #728 had "a number of trivial solutions to the problem as written"; the community reconstructed the intended statement on the forum before a Lean proof meant anything. #1026 was ambiguous from 1975. #124 has two readings of P(d,0) versus P(d,1).
  • Source error. #75: Erdős dropped an ℵ₁-vertices condition in one of his own papers; Aletheia proved the flawed version correctly. The curator cannot catch this, which is why author-attested is non-terminal in D-9.
  • Density. "Positive density" and "natural density" were formalized three different ways in #125, #741 and #424; AlphaProof exploited natural density before the statements were amended to lower or upper density. The #424 reporter asked for an audit of every "positive density" problem, which is still open.
  • Asymptotic notation. #394 had the sides of ≫ swapped for months; #728's n + O(log n) was the whole disagreement.
  • Junk values and guards. #480 said m ≠ 0 where it needed n ≠ 0; Aristotle proved it at n = 0 through Mathlib's 1/0 = 0. Bateman–Horn's tprod had the same shape in 2026.
  • Missing hypothesis. #859 lacked t ≥ 1; #56 lacked a constraint and fell to counterexamples "with tiny parameters like 2".
  • Definition mismatch. #918 used subgraph where induced subgraph was meant, found only when AlphaProof produced trivial solutions.

6. The seed filter, and how the trust score encodes it

A recorded fact pass over the registry, as F11-Q3 asks for. The catalog in §9 computes a heuristic trust score per problem from exactly these predicates; the "why" cell on each row lists which fired. The score is a triage aid derived from this report, not a certificate. D-9 certificates are still recorded per target by the fidelity command.

  1. Pin the registry. Import at an immutable tag or dated commit, never from main. The catalog is built at c7f31d5; record the commit in provenance.
  2. Erdős, research open, in an included domain. AMS 11 (number theory) or 05 (combinatorics), matching the protocol's target domains. Rows with no open Lean statement or a closed site status score n/a.
  3. Bloom-selected (+2, +1 more if the FC statement was vendored and asserted open at Epoch's pin). The problem is one of the 70 Bloom chose for FrontierMath Erdős. This is the only curated "significant and difficult" list that exists, made by the person who maintains the informal side.
  4. Survived the sweeps (+2). The statement was among the 353 AlphaProof Nexus attempted (Feb 2026) and is still open. This is evidence against trivial-reading defects. Aletheia's Dec 2025 literature search covered all 700 then-open problems, so "still open" also carries that check against a solution already in the literature.
  5. No misformalization issue (+1 if none ever; −2 if one is open). A closed one is neutral because the fixed version is what survived.
  6. Public long enough (+1 if the Lean file is 180+ days old; −1 if it changed in the last 30 days). This is D-9's N, read off the registry's history.
  7. Mathlib definitions only (+1). A file-local definition is a second review object under D-9 and the #918 failure mode.
  8. Wording hazards (−1 each, max −2): density, "sufficiently large", asymptotic notation, cardinal conditions, "almost all". These are the classes in §5.
  9. Value-typed (−1). A main statement of the form … = answer(sorry) is a question, not a conjecture; Epoch excluded these too.
  10. Site status lag (−2). The AI wiki records a full solution while the site still says open.

Letters: A at 6 or more points, B+ at exactly 5, B at 4, C at 2–3, D below. B+ and above is the network's high grade (F14): a proof under such a statement merges without a human reviewer, and below it a non-author approves the proof. Expect the A rows to be the Stage 0 shortlist; F11's default is five.

Then certify, cheaply

For each shortlisted problem, the D-9 mechanizable pass and one human act:

  • Triviality control: try to prove the statement and its negation with Lean automation and a short agent run. A cheap proof of either is a defect, not a result.
  • Two or three consequence or special-case lemmas from the informal statement, checked against the Lean.
  • Back-translation: an LLM informalizes the Lean without seeing the source; a non-author compares it to the erdosproblems.com wording. Compile-plus-two-judge agreement with human majority runs near 90% in the literature, so this is a screen, and the non-author's read is the certificate.
  • Record the external history as the published-and-uncontested evidence: days public in the registry, the named prover attempts, zero confirmed defects.

Decision for Mike D-9 says M independent proof attempts are earned "in public venues, not in-network". Counting AlphaProof Nexus, Aletheia and Aristotle runs on the registry as those attempts is consistent with that wording and is what makes the top grade reachable without an in-network expert. If you count them, the seeds enter at published-and-uncontested after a back-translation you can do yourself. If you do not, they enter at back-translated and stay there until three network provers have tried. Either way the floor for allocating compute is met.

7. What "no human reviewer" can honestly mean

It can mean no external expert, no hire, and no per-artifact approval click. It cannot mean nobody reads the statement, for two reasons the evidence forces. First, the dominant defect on open problems is an unintended reading of an ambiguous source, which no kernel and no lint can detect; a non-author back-translation is the cheapest known detector. Second, the registry you are inheriting from has no per-statement review record, so its own review cannot be cited as yours. The filter shrinks the human step to a comparison of two paragraphs per target.

9. Catalog: every Erdős problem with a Lean statement

One row per file in FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/ at the pinned commit. Columns and where each comes from:

  • #, prize: erdosproblems.com problem number (linked) and Erdős's prize as shown on the site.
  • Site status: the label on the site (OPEN, PROVED (LEAN), DISPROVED, …) with its explanatory line, the status database's last-update date, and the page's last-edited date. Open here means "Bloom is personally unaware of a solution".
  • Informal conjecture: the site's statement verbatim (LaTeX rendered); the expandable list gives every Lean declaration in the file with its category, AMS codes, docstring and any proof link.
  • Area: AMS codes from the Lean attributes, then the site's tags.
  • Standing since: earliest year among the site's citation keys, so "1974 (52 yr)" means the oldest source Bloom cites is from 1974. The first four keys are shown.
  • Lean: the file at the pinned commit, the main open statement's name, when the file was added to the registry, its last change, its commit count, and any file-local definitions. Toolchain and Mathlib for every row are the pin in the header.
  • Proof (closed problems): Lean proof links from formal_proof attributes on solved statements, and the solved statement's docstring, which names the informal proof and its reference.
  • Benchmarks: FrontierMath Erdős membership (FC-reviewed 48, autoformalized 17, or Bloom-selected but excluded as estimate-type), Bloom's top-10 list, and AlphaProof Nexus (attempted count, or the solved statement names).
  • Misformalization: every issue or PR in the registry labelled misformalization that names this problem, with state and dates.
  • AI wiki: rows from the Erdős-problems AI wiki (frozen 2026-06-30) for this problem, with section, systems, date and outcome.
  • Trust: the §6 score and its reasons.

10. Non-Erdős files with open statements

Every other file in the registry holding at least one research open statement, for completeness. These have no curator maintaining the informal side and no site status, so they carry only the registry's own intake review; the protocol's included domains and the §6 filter apply as written, minus the Bloom and Nexus predicates. Since F14 each open declaration is scored on its own: its statement text decides the local-definition and Mathlib predicates (three points when the statement is typed by Mathlib's own definition, listed in docs/mathlib_conjectures.yaml, one more for 180 days public in Mathlib), and OEIS Open loses three points, §2's "not eligible".
