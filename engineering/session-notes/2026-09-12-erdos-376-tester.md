# 2026-09-12 — a fresh agent tries to push the frontier of Erdős 376

An unattended session handed the network to a subagent with one instruction: *push the frontier
of Erdős problem 376*. It was given the website, the contributor guide (`AGENTS.md`), the HTTP
service and the MCP endpoint, and told to report bugs and missing features. This note records how
the test was staged, what the tester found, which of its findings the session verified against
the code and by replaying its writes through the pinned gate, and what the record and the site
did with them. Raw material is in `2026-09-12-erdos-376-tester/`:

## How it was staged

The session's container cannot reach `openproofnetwork.org` or `api.openproofnetwork.org`
(egress policy: CONNECT refused), and it has no Lean toolchain and no Docker daemon. GitHub is
reachable. So the live graph was cloned at `02a4e239` (main), the site was rendered from it with
the real generator and served on a loopback port, and the real service was run in process over a
memory store and the test suite's fake git host seeded with the clone — the same shape the
`AGENTS.md` walkthrough test uses — with one throwaway precheck key committed to the in-memory
copy. Consequences the tester was told about: every precheck "passes" at once and checks no Lean;
every write returns a `pr_url` that exists only in the fake host's records; nothing was pushed
anywhere. The tester ran for 23 minutes, 65 tool calls, and opened 23 pull requests on the fake
host, 47 pushes counting precheck jobs.

## What the tester did

Read the site (target, node, frontier, docs), then the guide; earned a token through the tutorial
precheck; tried to claim `erdos-376` (409, correctly: the target is `listed`); claimed the one
claimable node to exercise the lifecycle; filed an informal annex on the Kummer-carries reduction
(`C(2n,n)` coprime to `p` iff every base-`p` digit of `n` is below `p/2`); proposed a related
variant (the 3·5 case), a `partial`-labelled variant (the 5·7 case, with a `Relation.lean`), two
speculative cruxes (the Kummer reduction and the digit-set infinitude); wrote and submitted a
partial-proof skeleton with those two holes; filed a postmortem and an approach record; drove the
same surface through MCP; probed limits and refusals (bad enums, spoofed fields, oversized bodies,
wrong target, duplicate submissions, a sorry witness, the curator's pseudonym); and read the site
again. Its own verdict: the frontier of 376 moved by zero nodes, correctly, because the root is
`listed` and every artifact sits in an unmerged pull request.

## The replay: what the record and the site did with the 23 pull requests

`check.py` materialised each pull request as a branch off `main` and ran the pinned gate's own
`classify` over it. **All 23 classified as the tester intended and none was refused**: 9
`append`, 9 `proposal` (each naming its admission directory), 4 `partial`, 1 `proof` (the
skeleton sent at `Proof.lean`, see bug 4). The D-23 split held on every push: author the
pseudonym, committer `open-proof-network[bot]`. Every submission body carried its
`opn-precheck-attestation` block. The five building modes cannot be taken further without the
toolchain.

Three merges were replayed:

1. **The three genuine appends alone** (annex, postmortem, approach record) — **everything
   updated correctly.** `products` regenerated; the frontier entry for `erdos-376` went to
   `annex_present: true`, `attempts: 1`, `failure_class_histogram: {missing-library: 1}`; the
   node page shows the annex (escaped, labelled untrusted) and "1 recorded … missing-library 1";
   the target page lists the approach record; the frontier page's row updated.
2. **One related variant alone** — **products refuse**: `root is ambiguous: 2 nodes have no
   dependents (erdos-376, variant-e30ecfbb); declare one in targets/<id>/status/`. This is
   F08-Q19 again, and it is live: the five Erdős targets' `target-status/v2` records declare no
   `root` (only the tutorial's does, written after its first variant merged). **The first merged
   variant or crux on any of the five open targets fails the post-merge job**, the products stop
   rendering and the site freezes at the previous commit until a curator declares the root by
   pull request. The schema has the field; intake should write it.
3. **Everything non-building** (18 pull requests, four probes admission would refuse excluded) —
   products refuse for the same reason with four sinks. With the probes included they refuse
   earlier, on `spec-6d0f878a` importing another node's `Context` — which is the right refusal,
   but it shows the classifier passes what only admission catches. In both cases the **site
   still rendered** (32 pages) from the tree plus the stale committed products: the node page
   showed the new annexes and attempts while `graph.json` and the frontier knew nothing of the
   new nodes. A failed post-merge job is invisible on the site.

Two more things the replay showed that the tester could not see:

- **The append-name collision loses a record.** Three postmortems filed within one second all
  named `attempts/20260912T225733Z-agent-062b0f.yaml`. Merged in order, the last (a probe)
  overwrote the first (the real one, `missing-library`, the annex cited). On the live host the
  second and third become modifications after the first merges and the gate refuses them — but
  their authors were told 201 and given a `pr_url`, and the file name is a second-resolution
  timestamp while the service already mints a ULID per record (`appends.py` documents the
  collision as intended).
- **The tester's own annex is numerically wrong and it said so.** Its aside lists 1, 2, 10, 3160,
  3161 as members; the session recomputed: below 25000 the members are 1, 10, 756, 757, 3160,
  3186, 3187, 3250, 7560, 7561, 7651, 20007 (by the digit test; the direct `gcd(C(2n,n), 105)`
  computation agrees with it on every `n` below 4000). The two annexes (`797238e9…`, `271695b9…`) should not
  be merged as they stand. Nothing in the pipeline would have caught this; D-31 says an annex is
  untrusted prose, and it is.

## What a contributor to 376 wanted and did not find

In the tester's order of importance: the intake's prior art and attack routes (`target.yaml`
has EGRS 1975, the AlphaProof attempt and the Kummer route; no page, product or tool shows
them); a claimability explanation with the path to claimable; a way to see its own pending
contributions and their gate status; a toolchain-free dry run for proposals and partials that
reports the node id and the children a skeleton would create; the schemas the service validates
against; idempotent writes; a guide section that submits a partial end to end; and, for 376
itself, a curator-seeded decomposition, since the Kummer reduction is routine and the two-prime
cases (theorems since 1975) are provable today.

## Lessons

- A staged bench over the fakes gets a real tester through the whole surface in twenty minutes
  with nothing pushed; the replay through `classify` and `products` is what turned its 23 PRs
  into findings the tester could not see (the root ambiguity, the lost postmortem).
- Every mode's first live use on a *new target* re-finds what the first live use on the tutorial
  found. The tutorial's root declaration was written by hand after F08-Q19; intake should write
  one for every target it takes in.
