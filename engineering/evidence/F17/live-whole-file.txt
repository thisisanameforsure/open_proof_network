### Proof Plan

We are given the theorem:

    `∀ p q : Prop, p ∧ q → p`

This is a simple propositional logic statement that says: For any propositions `p` and `q`, if `p ∧ q` holds, then `p` holds.

#### Step 1: Understand the Goal
The goal is to prove `∀ p q : Prop, p ∧ q → p`. Breaking this down:
.
- We have universal quantifiers over propositions `p` and `q`.
- The premise is `p ∧ q`, meaning both `p` and `q` are true.
- The conclusion is just `p`.

#### Step 2: Proof Strategy
In Lean, we can prove this by:
1. Introducing the propositions `p` and `q` (using `intro` for ∀).
2. Assuming we have a proof `h : p ∧ q`.
3. Extracting the left component from `h` (i.e., the proof of `p`).

#### Step 3: Available Tactics/Constructs
Since we are in Lean core (no Mathlib), we can use:
1. `intro` to introduce ∀ variables and implications.
2. `rcases` or `cases` to destruct the conjunction.
3. Alternatively, `have` with `.left` or `.1` to extract the first component of a conjunction.

#### Step 4: Possible Approaches
- **Approach A:** Use `intro p q h`, then `exact h.left`.
-
- **Approach B:** Use `intro p q h`, then `cases h with | intro hp hq => exact hp`.
.
- **Approach C:** Use λ-abstractions: `λ p q h => h.left`.

#### Step 5: Final Lean Code Structure
We'll write:
```
  intro p q h
  exact h.left
```
because `h : p ∧ q` has fields `.left` (or `.1`) for `p` and `.right` (or `.2`) for `q`.
