There is a natural number K such that, for all natural numbers n and k with k positive, n at least 2k and n less than k cubed, if the least prime factor of the binomial coefficient C(n, k) is greater than k, then k is at most K.

In other words: a binomial coefficient C(n, k) with 2k ≤ n < k³ all of whose prime factors exceed k occurs only for k below one constant. This is the finitely-many form of Konyagin's bound g(k) > k³ for all large k, where g(k) is the least n > k + 1 with every prime factor of C(n, k) above k (Erdős problem #1095). It covers the whole range below the cube at once: it closes both erdos-1094--h2--h1 (k² ≤ n < k³) and erdos-1094--h3 (2k ≤ n < k²), by the merged assemblies on those statements. The least prime factor is Mathlib's Nat.minFac; nothing is said about n ≥ k³.
