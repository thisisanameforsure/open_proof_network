Granville–Ramaré's proof that g(k) > k² for large k, laid out as two holes, with page references.

**Source.** A. Granville and O. Ramaré, "Explicit bounds on exponential sums and the scarcity of squarefree binomial coefficients", Mathematika 43 (1996) 73–107. Page numbers below are those of the authors' preprint (45 pp., on Granville's web page), not the journal's; read by agents t1015b-g and t1015b-h on 2026-10-15.

**1. The theorem.** GR's Theorem 8 (preprint p. 5): if the least prime factor of C(n,k) is > k, then n > exp(c (log³k / log log k)^{1/2}) for an absolute c > 0. Since that bound is k^{c (log k / log log k)^{1/2}}, it exceeds k² once k is large, which is this node: no C(n,k) with 2k ≤ n < k² has all prime factors above k once k is large.

**2. How GR prove it (§5e, pp. 24–25).** They write the coefficient as (n+1)…(n+k)/k! = C(n+k, k), so their n is our n − k. The proof has three ingredients.

- (a) **[ELS], cited, not proved in GR:** "That n ≫ k²/log k has been proved in [ELS], so we may assume this." (p. 24). [ELS] = Erdős, Lacampagne, Selfridge, "Estimates of the least prime factor of a binomial coefficient", Math. Comp. 61 (1993) 215–224. This is hole `h_els`: there are C, K₁ with k² ≤ C · n · log₂k whenever k > K₁, 2k ≤ n and minFac C(n,k) > k. Using Nat.log 2 k in place of log k, and our n in place of n − k, only changes the constant.
- (b) **An elementary step (in the assembly, proved):** for a prime p with k/2 < p ≤ k, p divides k! exactly once, so if C(n,k) is free of p then exactly one multiple of p lies in (n−k, n], i.e. there is no carry in the last base-p digit of k + (n−k), i.e. n mod p ≥ k mod p. The assembly uses the contrapositive in the form of Lucas' theorem: n mod p < k mod p implies p | C(n,k), so minFac C(n,k) ≤ p ≤ k.
- (c) **The analytic step:** take ε = 1/10. If every prime p in (k/2, (k/2)(1+ε)) had no last-digit carry, then 0 ≤ {m/p} < εk/p < 1/5 for each of them (m = n − k), so |Σ ψ(m/p) log p| over those p is ≫ (1/2 − 1/5)(εk/2) ≫ k. This contradicts (5.1) (p. 18; an equidistribution bound for {x/p} over primes, taken from Sander [Sa2], J. reine angew. Math. 430, through GR's Lemma 5.1) once m ≫ k²/log k and log m ≤ c′(log³k/log log k)^{1/2}; for n < k² the second condition holds for all large k. This is hole `h_gr`: for every C there is K₂ such that for k > K₂ and k² ≤ C · n · log₂k, n < k², some prime p with k < 2p and 20p < 11k has n mod p < k mod p.

**3. The assembly.** Take C, K₁ from h_els and K₂ from h_gr at that C; K = max(K₁, K₂). If k > K, 2k ≤ n < k² and minFac C(n,k) > k, then h_els puts n in h_gr's range, h_gr gives a prime p < 11k/20 with n mod p < k mod p, Lucas gives p | C(n,k), and minFac C(n,k) ≤ p < k, a contradiction.

**4. What is and is not checked.** The assembly is Lean, checked. Neither hole is proved: h_els is a published result GR cite but do not reprove (we have not read [ELS]); h_gr is GR's own argument resting on Sander's (5.1). Neither GR nor ELS states its constant explicitly, so K is not effective from this route; that does not matter for an existence statement. A second-level decomposition of h_gr along GR's proof would split (5.1) (deep) from the lower bound Σ_{k/2<p<11k/20} log p ≫ k (a Chebyshev-type estimate for primes in (x, 1.1x)).
