F05-T20 — the claims registry stops scanning the whole claims table on every anonymous read
(audit 2026-10-04). Worktree opn-audit-cb-reads; fast tier only.

== The finding ==
frontier.registry called store.list_claims (DynamoStore: a paged Scan of every claim ever made;
R9's history_count keeps released and expired rows) on every GET /frontier.json and
GET /claims.json, plus one get_identity per active holder. An anonymous poller's cost grew with
the table.

== Seams added first, at today's behaviour ==
Setting claims_max_stale_s (unused), Context.claims_scan (always None).

(The red run also carried a ttl-epoch test, removed with the ttl work on the owner's ruling below.)

== The fix ==
  - frontier.claims_scan: the rows and the holders' pseudonyms kept on Context.claims_scan
    (app.CachedClaims) for claims_max_stale_s (default 30, OPN_API_CLAIMS_MAX_STALE_S). registry
    still decides which claims are active against the clock on every read, so caching never
    extends a claim past expires (R8).
  - claims.invalidate(ctx) after the two claim writes (POST /claims create, DELETE /claims/<id>
    release). Another Lambda instance sees a write within the window.
  - No ttl attribute: the owner ruled 2026-10-04 that the claims table keeps its full history
    (no TTL), so history_count stays all-time.

process's claim writes (audit 2026-10-04; evidence task-20-audit-2026-10-04.txt).

Proposed Q (next free F05 Q): "R9's registry is served from a scan of the claims table kept for
claims_max_stale_s (default 30 s), not a scan per read; which claims are active is decided per
read against the clock, so R8's lazy expiry is exact. A claim or release through a process drops
its kept scan; another process may serve the registry up to the window late, which the
registry's snapshot_at already admits. The table keeps every row (owner, 2026-10-04: no TTL), so
history_count stays all-time; the scan's cost still grows with the table, and only the read rate
is bounded."
