$$\exists K_1\ \forall n,k\in\mathbb{N}:\quad K_1<k,\ \ 2k\le n<k^2,\ \ k \text{ odd},\ \ n \text{ odd},\ \ 3\mid k \implies \exists\,p\le k \text{ prime with } n \bmod p < k \bmod p$$

For all large odd $k$ divisible by $3$, every odd $n$ with $2k\le n<k^2$ has a prime $p\le k$ whose residue of $n$ is smaller than its residue of $k$. Lean writes oddness as `k % 2 = 1` and `n % 2 = 1`, $3\mid k$ as `k % 3 = 0`, and $k^2$ as `k * k`. `n % p` is the least non-negative residue, so the condition reads $n \bmod p < k \bmod p$, a carry at the last base-$p$ digit when $k$ is subtracted from $n$ (hence $p \mid \binom{n}{k}$ by Kummer's theorem, though the statement names no binomial coefficient).
