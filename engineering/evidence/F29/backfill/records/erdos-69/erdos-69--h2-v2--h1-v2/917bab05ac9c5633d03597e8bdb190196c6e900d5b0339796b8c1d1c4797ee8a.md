$$\Bigl(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(n)}{2^{n}} = \sum_{p\ \text{prime}}\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}\Bigr) \Rightarrow \forall b\in\mathbb{N},\ b>0\ \ \exists N\in\mathbb{N}\ \ \forall z\in\mathbb{Z}:\ b\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(N+1+j)}{2^{j+1}} \ne z$$

If the series of ω(n)/2^n over n ≥ 0 equals the sum over primes p of 1/(2^p − 1), then for every positive natural b there is a natural N such that b times the rescaled tail ∑_{j≥0} ω(N+1+j)/2^{j+1} is not an integer, everything in ℝ with ω(n) the number of distinct prime factors of n. Every series is Mathlib's `tsum` over ℝ, which is 0 when the family is not summable.
