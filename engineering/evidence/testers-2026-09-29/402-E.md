## Mathematics

**What h3-v2 asks, in plain mathematics.** Its two leading hypotheses are the statements of the
proved holes h1 (Farey structure) and h2-v2 (reduction to gcd 1); both are true and unused. What
remains: for every finite set A of positive integers with gcd(A) = 1, n = |A|, such that every
ratio a/b (a, b ∈ A) in lowest terms u/v has u, v ≤ n, there are a, b ∈ A with
gcd(a, b) ≤ a/n, i.e. a/gcd(a, b) ≥ n. If the conclusion fails, all a/gcd(a, b) ≤ n − 1, so the
ratio hypothesis holds automatically: h3-v2 is Graham's conjecture for primitive sets, and with
h2-v2 the whole of Erdős 402. `root → h3-v2` is a two-line specialisation (checked by /check,
`402-E/root_implies_h3.lean`).

**Numerics** (Python, exhaustive, `402-E/numerics.py`, `402-E/numerics.out`; not Lean-checked).
Over all n-subsets of {1..N} for (n, N) = (2,60) … (8,26), and all n-subsets of divisors of
lcm(1..n) for n ≤ 8: the sets meeting h3-v2's hypotheses are exactly Graham's equality cases
{1..n}, {L/n, …, L/1} (L = lcm(1..n)), and for n = 4 also {2,3,4,6}, {3,4,6,12}; all have
max a/gcd = n exactly; no conclusion failure.

**Judgement: (c), leaning (a).** h3-v2 carries the full Balasubramanian–Soundararajan theorem
(it is implied by the root by specialisation), but two slices of it are elementary and are now
proved in Lean inside a skeleton (checked by /check only, not prechecked):
1. prime-power bound: in a primitive counterexample every prime power dividing an element is < n
   (so every element divides lcm(1..n−1));
2. |A| = p prime: a ↦ a mod p into p − 1 non-zero residues; a collision a = g x, b = g y with
   x, y < p, p ∤ g forces x = y;
3. |A| = p + 1, p prime: residue of the p-free part mod p; a collision is {c, p c}; two such pairs
   {c, pc}, {d, pd} force c | d and d | c.
The skeleton's one hole is the bound for sizes n that are neither prime nor prime + 1
(9, 10, 15, 16, 21, 22, 25, …), with a ≤ n·gcd(a, b) and all prime powers < n as hypotheses;
witness {1,…,10} (checked by /check in witness mode against my rendering of the hole).
For n = p + 2 a residue class can hold three elements ({c, pc, (p+1)c}), so the argument needs a
cross-class step; that is where the literature's case analysis by primes in short intervals
begins (worked out informally, not formalised).
