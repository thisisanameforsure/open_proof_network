Proof (checked by /check, mode check, okay true; not yet prechecked): convert the `Nat.Primes` sum to an indicator sum over ℕ with `tsum_subtype`; with r = 1/b, if the sum were q with denominator k, take N = (k+1)! + 1; the k places after N are composite (j+2 divides (k+1)! + j + 2); the tail R after place N+1+k is positive (a prime beyond it) and at most 2·r^{N+1+k}; b^N·(finite part) is an integer Z; so b^N·num − k·Z = k·b^N·R lies in (0, k/b^k) ⊂ (0,1), impossible for an integer.
