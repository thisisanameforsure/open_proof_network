Write T_N for the tail ∑' j, ω(N+1+j)/2^(j+1) and S = ∑_{j<k} ω(N+1+j)·2^(k-1-j). Its own second hypothesis (tail recursion) says 2^k·T_N = S + T_{N+k}. If b·T_N were an integer z, then b·S + b·T_{N+k} = z·2^k, and with 0 < T_{N+k} ≤ log₂(N+k+1)+1 (its third and fourth hypotheses) that forces (b·S mod 2^k) + b·(log₂(N+k+1)+1) ≥ 2^k for every k. So the crux's conclusion "∃ N k, (b·S mod 2^k) + b(log₂(N+k+1)+1) < 2^k" is, given its hypotheses, the parent's "∃ N, b·T_N ∉ ℤ" in quantitative form: the record's `circular` mark is right, and closing it is the whole of Erdős's argument (in plby/lean-proofs, the composite-dilation files), not a lemma.
