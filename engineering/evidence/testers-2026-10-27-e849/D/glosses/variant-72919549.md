$$\forall N \in \mathbb{N}\ \ \exists a \ge N:\quad 3 \le \#\bigl\{\, n \in \mathbb{N} : \exists k \ge 1,\ 2k \le n,\ \tbinom{n}{k} = a \,\bigr\}$$

Here $N$, $a$, $n$, $k$ are natural numbers, and $2k \le n$ is the range $1 \le k \le n/2$ written without division. The set collects **rows** $n$ of Pascal's triangle, counted once each; its size is `Set.ncard`, which is $0$ for an infinite set as well as an empty one (every such set lies in $[2, a]$, the statement spec-38faff03). The bound is "at least three", inclusive.

In words: for every $N$ there is some $a \ge N$ that appears as $\binom{n}{k}$ with $1 \le k \le n/2$ in at least three rows. That is, infinitely many $a$ lie in three or more rows of the half triangle.
