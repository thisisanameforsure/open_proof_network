$$\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N},\ p \in \mathbb{Z},\ q \ge 1:\qquad \sum_{a\in A}\frac{1}{2^a-1} = \frac{p}{q}\ \Longrightarrow\ \forall j \ge 0:\quad q\,T_j \;=\; p\,2^{j} - q\sum_{m=0}^{j} f_A(m)\,2^{\,j-m},$$
where $f_A(m) = \#\{a \in A : a \mid m\}$ for $m \ge 1$, $f_A(0) = 0$, and $T_j = \sum_{i \ge 1} \dfrac{f_A(j+i)}{2^{i}}$ is the tail of the binary expansion $\sum_{m\ge1} f_A(m)/2^m$ after position $j$, scaled by $2^j$.

In words: if the sum is rational with denominator $q$, then $q$ times every tail $T_j$ is an integer, and the integer is written out. On the right, $f_A(m)$ is `Set.ncard {a ∈ A | 0 < m ∧ a ∣ m}` (the guard makes $f_A(0)=0$), $2^{j-m}$ is a natural-number power with $m \le j$ so the subtraction is exact, and the finite sum is Lean's `Finset.sum` over `Finset.range (j+1)`, that is $m = 0, \dots, j$. On the left the tail is `∑' i, ncard {a ∈ A | a ∣ j + i + 1} / 2^(i+1)`, the ordinary sum of a convergent series ($f_A(m) \le d(m) \le m$). The hypothesis writes the rational as `(p : ℝ) / (q : ℝ)` with `q : ℕ`; the statement says nothing when $q = 0$ because of the hypothesis $0 < q$. A set containing $0$ changes neither side ($\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$ in Lean, and $0$ divides no $m \ge 1$).
