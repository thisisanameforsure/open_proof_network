$$\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N},\ p \in \mathbb{Z},\ q \ge 1,\ y, k \ge 0:\qquad \sum_{a\in A}\frac{1}{2^a-1} = \frac{p}{q}\ \ \text{and}\ \ 2^k \mid f_A(y+i)\ \text{for } i = 1,\dots,k\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \exists\, N \in \mathbb{Z}:\ q\,T_{y+k} = 2^k N,$$
where $f_A(m) = \#\{a \in A : a \mid m\}$ and $T_{j} = \sum_{i \ge 1} \dfrac{f_A(j+i)}{2^{i}}$ is the tail of the binary expansion $\sum_{m \ge 1} f_A(m)/2^m$ after position $j$, scaled by $2^j$.

In words: if the sum is rational with denominator $q$, and the $k$ coefficients just after position $y$ are all divisible by $2^k$ (in particular if they all vanish), then $q$ times the tail after the block is an integer multiple of $2^k$. The block condition is `∀ i, 1 ≤ i → i ≤ k → 2 ^ k ∣ Set.ncard {a ∈ A | a ∣ y + i}`, positions $y+1, \dots, y+k$ inclusive; with $k = 0$ it is empty and the conclusion only says $q\,T_y$ is an integer. The tail is `∑' i, ncard {a ∈ A | a ∣ y + k + i + 1} / 2^(i+1)`, the ordinary sum of a convergent series ($f_A(m) \le d(m) \le m$). The hypothesis writes the rational as `(p : ℝ) / (q : ℝ)` with `q : ℕ` and $0 < q$. The statement does not say the multiple is nonzero: for infinite $A$ the tail is positive, so $N \ge 1$ and $q\,T_{y+k} \ge 2^k$, but that step is not part of this statement.
