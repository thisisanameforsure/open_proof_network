## Overview

Write $e(n) = \frac{1}{2^n-1}$ (Lean's $e(0) = \frac10 = 0$) and $f_A(m) = \#\{a \in A : 0 < m,\ a \mid m\}$. The claim is $\sum_{n \in A} e(n) = \sum_{m \ge 0} f_A(m)/2^m$ for every $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$. The proof expands each $e(n)$, $n \ge 1$, as the geometric series $\sum_{j \ge 0} 2^{-n(j+1)}$, so that the left side is a double series over pairs $(n, j)$ with $n \in A$, $n \ge 1$; it sums that double series once row by row (giving the left side) and once along the fibres of $(n, j) \mapsto n(j+1)$ (giving the right side, since the fibre over $m \ge 1$ has one point for each divisor $n$ of $m$). Both regroupings are legitimate because the terms are nonnegative and the row sums are summable. The private helper lemmas before the theorem are the facts $0 \le e(n) \le 2 \cdot 2^{-n}$, the summability of $e$, and the geometric expansion $e(n) = \sum_{j \ge 0} (2^{-n})^{j+1}$ for $n \ge 1$.

## The double series and its summability {steps: h0B hg_nonneg hrow hrow_summable he_summable hg_summable}

Put $B = A \cap \{n : n > 0\}$, so $0 \notin B$ (h0B), and define $g(n, j) = \mathbf{1}_B(n)\,(2^{-n})^{j+1}$ on $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$. Every $g(n, j) \ge 0$ (hg_nonneg). For $n \in B$ the row $j \mapsto g(n, j)$ is the geometric series with ratio $2^{-n} < 1$, so it is summable and its sum is $e(n)$ by the helper lemma; for $n \notin B$ the row is identically $0$. Hence $\sum_j g(n, j) = \mathbf{1}_B(n)\,e(n)$ for every $n$ (hrow) and every row is summable (hrow_summable). Since $0 \le e(n) \le 2 \cdot 2^{-n}$, $e$ is summable (he_summable), so its restriction $\mathbf{1}_B e$ is, and by the criterion for nonnegative functions on a product (`summable_prod_of_nonneg`: every row summable and the row sums summable) $g$ is summable on $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ (hg_summable).

## The left side as the row-by-row sum {steps: hL hmid}

The sum over the subtype $A$ is the sum over $\mathbb{N}$ of $\mathbf{1}_A e$, and $\mathbf{1}_A e = \mathbf{1}_B e$ pointwise: they agree at every $n \ne 0$ because $n \in B \iff n \in A$ there, and at $n = 0$ both are $0$ since $e(0) = 0$ (hL). For a summable function on a product, the sum equals the iterated sum over rows (`Summable.tsum_prod`), and the row sums were computed in hrow, so $\sum_{n} \mathbf{1}_B(n) e(n) = \sum_{(n,j)} g(n, j)$ (hmid).

## The right side as the sum along the fibres {steps: hfib hR}

Let $\varphi(n, j) = n(j+1)$. For a summable $g$, the fibre sums $m \mapsto \sum_{p \in \varphi^{-1}(m)} g(p)$ form a family whose sum is $\sum_p g(p)$ (`HasSum.tsum_fiberwise`, hfib). It remains to compute each fibre sum (hR). Over $m = 0$ the fibre is $\{(0, j)\}$ and $g(0, j) = 0$ because $0 \notin B$; the right side's term at $m = 0$ is $0$ too, since the counted set is empty. Over $m \ge 1$: a pair in the fibre with $g(n, j) \ne 0$ has $n \in B$, so $n \ge 1$, $n \mid m$ and $j = m/n - 1$; thus the fibre sum is supported on the finite set $T = \{(d, m/d - 1) : d \mid m\}$, the map $d \mapsto (d, m/d - 1)$ is injective, and the sum over $T$ is $\sum_{d \mid m} \mathbf{1}_A(d)\,(2^{-d})^{m/d} = \sum_{d \mid m} \mathbf{1}_A(d)\, 2^{-m}$ (for $d \mid m$, $d \ge 1$ one has $d \cdot (m/d) = m$, and $d \in B \iff d \in A$). That is $\#\{d \mid m : d \in A\} \cdot 2^{-m}$, and the set $\{a \in A : 0 < m,\ a \mid m\}$ is exactly the finset of divisors of $m$ lying in $A$, so its `ncard` is that count. Chaining hL, hmid and hR closes the theorem.
