== The change (graph gate.yml, postmerge_batch.py helper and the post-merge job) ==

  publish(..., render=False, follow=...): the first push is unchanged (record + products, `pushed`).
    On a refusal: fetch, drop what main already credits (`owed`), replay on a re-pin (`pin_moved`)
    or a record that does not apply — all as before — else rebuild(new, pairs, products=render):
    for a merge, check out main as it is, `git apply --3way --index record.patch`, merge the tag
    caches, write the ledger lines again, commit `gate: #N <verdict>` — no PRODUCTS_CMD. A push
    that then lands answers `recorded` and calls follow(): `gh workflow run render.yml` with the
    job's own github.token (actions: write, already granted; no new secret, C8). A failed dispatch
    is a ::warning:: and the step stays green (C7). render.yml's own loop (render=True) still
    renders on every attempt (it has nothing but products).
  PRODUCTS_CMD removed from the commit step's env (the catch-up never renders); render.yml keeps its own.
  The commit step has `id: record` and writes recorded=<sha>: HEAD after `pushed`/`recorded`, the
    newest first-parent commit crediting the run's merges after `credited` (credited_at), nothing
    after `replayed`/`gave-up`. Job output `recorded`.
  Cache job (F32-T6's finding, the coordinator's message): checkout ref and COMMIT env are
    `${{ needs.postmerge.outputs.recorded || needs.postmerge.outputs.merge }}`, publish uses
    --commit "$COMMIT". Choice: `recorded` is the commit the RECORD landed in (the first push's
    record+products commit, or the record-only catch-up commit), never the later render commit;
    the record commit is the first commit on main holding a born-proved hole's files, and any
    fetch takes the newest cache at or before its commit.

Why render.yml rather than a second commit in the post-merge job: a second render-commit inside the
job would meet the same race the record just escaped (a 76 s render, refused when main moves) and
would keep the post-merge job, and its red/green, hostage to it. render.yml is decoupled: its
concurrency group `render` (cancel-in-progress: false) holds one running and one pending run, so a
burst of record-only catch-ups costs at most one render after the one in flight, and a render
always starts after the record that dispatched it, so it renders a tree that holds it. Its own
retry loop is the same 76 s race (see the estimate below): under a sustained burst a render can give
up (red), and the pending render queued by later catch-ups lands at the next quiet window. Every
merge whose first push lands also carries complete products. So the products converge once main is
quiet for one render; the record no longer waits for that.

== Risks ==

  1. A window, after a catch-up, where main's products do not show a landed attestation (seconds
     to minutes; under a sustained burst until main is quiet for one ~80 s render). Consumers that
     read products (site, frontier, the service) show the merge late in that window; the record is
     not late. The service's F06-T18 rule treats the record commit as a render in flight for
     hole-making prechecks on that target, as intended.
  2. render.yml under a burst: its loop re-renders per refusal and can give up red after six
     (the sim's render 1). Harmless to the record (it credits nothing), but a red run nobody needs
     to act on. If the only things moving main were outline commits (which dispatch no render), a
     render that gave up would leave the products stale until the next merge or render; not seen on
     the live schedule. Not built (unrequested): letting a render give up quietly when a newer
     render is pending.
  3. The record-only commit keeps the subject `gate: #N <verdict>`; anything reading that subject
     as "the products of #N are in this commit" is wrong after this change. Searched: the helper's
     credits/owed, postmerge.py's bot-message regex, merge.yml's lane rule (record-only commits touch
     nodes/ exactly as today's gate commits do; render commits touch products only) — none assumes it.
  4. Not run: the lean and docker tiers (no Lean or sandbox path changed), and nothing live.
