Not universal, and that is the point: it depends on the generated statement's shape.
The four euclid-primes holes are proved with no acknowledged_hazards; the tutorial's two
and erdos-412--h1 are blocked.

Why it cannot be fixed from the contributor side: Statement.lean is immutable (D-3) and
META.yaml is a path no submission may touch, so only intake or a curator revision can add
the acknowledgement. And since F03-T9 the hole is CLAIMABLE, so the frontier now advertises
work that cannot pass step 6.

The fix is a design choice and is the owner's, not this session's:
  (a) the post-merge job runs step 6 over what it writes and refuses to create a node it
      would reject - but then the merge has nowhere to put the hole;
  (b) a gate-authored statement's findings are acknowledged by the gate, with a
      justification naming the merge that derived it (a machine claim, not a human one);
  (c) the hole is created with a cause of its own (hazards-unacknowledged) and is not
      claimable until a curator acknowledges - honest, but it re-blocks what F03-T9 opened.

--- CONTROL: why the euclid holes passed, and the erdos one will not -------

The four euclid-primes holes are proved with no acknowledged_hazards, on a gate-spec that DOES
enable off-by-one-range. That looked like a contradiction, so it was tested rather than assumed.

infinitude-of-primes--h1's statement is

    theorem infinitude_of_primes__h1 : forall (n k : N), 0 < k -> k <= n -> Opn.Divides k n.factorial

The same bounds, Mathlib-free, through the same step 6 and the same checker list:

    theorem ... : forall (n k : Nat), 0 < k -> k <= n -> k <= n + 1
    verdict: pass   findings: NONE

So the checker flags a comparison against an explicit literal (2 <= x, 3 <= m) and does not flag
`0 <` or a variable-to-variable comparison. That is why every euclid hole passed, and it is the
control that makes the erdos-412--h1 result mean what it says: three real findings, none
acknowledged, on a target whose spec runs the checker.
