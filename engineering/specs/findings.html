Findings register — the Erdős 376 tester pass (2026-09-12)

An agent that had never seen the network was handed the website, the contributor guide, the HTTP service and the MCP endpoint, and told to push the frontier of Erdős problem 376. It opened 23 pull requests against a staged bench and reported 23 defects and 11 missing features; this session replayed every one of those pull requests through the pinned gate and re-rendered the site from the result. This page is the triage: each finding assigned to the feature that owns it, so the work enters the ledger rather than a narrative. The raw material — the brief, the tester's verbatim report, the bench, the replay and its outputs — is engineering/session-notes/2026-09-12-erdos-376-tester.md and the directory beside it.

how to read this: severity is about the record, not about effort. live acts on the mathematical record as it stands today; blocks stops a contributor who is doing everything right; record loses or corrupts what someone filed; friction and cosmetic cost patience only. “Tester #n” cites that finding's number in the report. Task numbers are deliberately unassigned: the session that takes one gives it the next free number in its feature and writes the requirement there, because a number promised here and spent elsewhere is exactly the rot the build log warns about.

1. Take this one first

The first merged variant or crux on any of the five open Erdős targets stops the products. (live · owner's act · F11 intake, F03 root inference · tester found the mechanism, the replay found that it is armed) A D-30 variant and a D-14 speculative crux both have no dependents, so the moment either merges the target's DAG has two sinks and F03's root inference refuses — the post-merge job writes nothing, the products freeze at the previous commit, and the site goes on serving the stale frontier with no sign that anything failed. This is F08-Q19 exactly, and it was paid once already on the tutorial target, whose targets/tutorial/status/2026-09-11-1.yaml declares root: tutorial-and-swap by hand after the fact. None of erdos-52, erdos-68, erdos-172, erdos-376 or erdos-406 carries that declaration, and all five are one accepted proposal away from needing it. Replayed: merging a single related variant onto erdos-376 gives root is ambiguous: 2 nodes have no dependents (erdos-376, variant-e30ecfbb); declare one in targets/<id>/status/; the site still renders 32 pages from the stale products, so the failure is invisible where the public looks. Two fixes, and they are not alternatives. (a) The graph act, which is the owner's and cannot be done from this repo: file a target-status/v2 record declaring the root on each of the five, the way the tutorial's was filed. (b) The tooling act, which is a spec task here: intake new should write the root declaration at listing, so no target is ever seeded into this state again — and F03's inference should not count a node whose META.yaml origin is variant as a candidate root, which is the change F08-Q19 said “belongs with F12's target-level records” and was never taken.

3. Loses or corrupts a record

FindingOwnerWhat the fix looks like
Two appends in the same second share a file name, and the loser is told 201. (tester #6; the replay lost a real postmortem to a probe) Append names are <timestamp>-<pseudonym> at second resolution (api/opn_api/appends.py), so three postmortems filed in one second all became attempts/20260912T225733Z-agent-062b0f.yaml. Each is its own branch off main, so all three are additions until the first merges; merged in order, the last one wins and the first is gone. The module documents this as “the gate then rejects the second as a modification”, which is true only after the first merge, and is not what the contributor was told. An annex is worse: identical text hashes identically, so a retry is indistinguishable from a second record. F07 (appends) The service already mints a ULID per record and returns it as id. Put it in the file name, or refuse the second write when the path is taken on main or in an open pull request.
The annex citation is checked only after the merge. (tester #14; confirmed: the only callers of check_annex_citation are in gate/opn_gate/postmerge.py) D-31 says a skeleton citing an annex that is not on the node is rejected. classify answers ok: true for a skeleton citing forty zeros and for one with a malformed short hash, and nothing in the pipeline's steps calls the check — so a mis-cited skeleton passes the gate, merges into attempts/, and then the post-merge job raises. That is the worst moment for it: the merge has happened, the holes are not created, and a post-merge run that fails before its ledger step loses that line for good (the build log records this). F07 (partial mode) / F02-style step-6 placement Run the citation check pre-merge as the artifact step's last word, where the offload rule already lives (steps/artifact.py). It reads two files and needs no toolchain.
The curator's own pseudonym can be minted by anyone. (tester #8) A tutorial proof bought a token under the pseudonym thisisanameforsure — the single login in the graph's curators.json and the gate owner named in every gate-spec.json — and that identity then filed a postmortem attributed to the curator and held a claim shown under the curator's name on the frontier. Uniqueness is enforced against the service's own store and nothing else; the names the graph reserves are not reserved. D-21's rule that curators may not claim proof credit on targets they curate is enforced nowhere the tester or this session could find. F05 (identity), D-21 enforcement is a protocol question Read the reserved names from the graph (curators.json, each spec's gate_owner) at minting time. D-21 needs a decision about where it binds — the ledger step is the natural place — and that is the owner's call, not a task.
A number-theory variant was accepted onto the tutorial target. (tester #9; the replay classified it proposal on tutorial with no complaint) Nothing checks that a related variant bears on the target it is filed against; D-30 v3.12 leans on a later relevance signature, which is a human act after the node exists. F08 (admission), F12-R13 (relevance) At minimum refuse a proposal whose declaration namespace or import set cannot belong to that target. Otherwise the frontier carries nodes that no filter policy can sensibly rank, and the relevance signature arrives too late to prevent the noise.

4. Friction

FindingOwnerNote
Refused writes are charged against the hourly write budget. (tester #19) ratelimit.enforce bumps the counter before it compares, so 54 rejected claims on a nonexistent node exhausted 120 writes/hour and locked the identity out for 3274 seconds. A contributor debugging a request body pays for an hour of silence. F05-R6 Charge on the write, not on the attempt — or give 4xx its own, looser bucket.
No idempotency anywhere. (tester #7) The identical annex, variant and crux each opened a fresh pull request; one precheck job was spent on four separate submissions. Node ids collide by name only at merge time. F07, F08 Return the existing pull request for content already on main or in flight; consume a precheck job on first use.
One identity may stack claims on one node. (tester #18) Twenty simultaneous claims by one pseudonym on one node, all 201, which defeats the guide's own published filter policy for everyone else. F05 (claims) A second claim by the same identity should extend the first, not add one.
MCP argument names are not the endpoints' field names. (tester #12) ttl not ttl_hours; stmt not statement; attestation (an object) not precheck_job_id; and submit_postmortem takes only an object where the HTTP route takes a YAML string, refusing it with a raw validator message. The guide's appendix maps tool names to paths and says every tool is one of the calls, which reads as a promise about arguments. F09 (bijection), F10 (the appendix) Either align the names or state the mapping per argument. The demarcation and auth behaviour were otherwise exactly as D-28 describes.
Attestation ids are zero-padded and nothing says so. (tester #13) Submissions return pr_number: 7; the file is attestations/000007.json; get_submission("7") misses. A merged proposal has no attestation at all and the tool cannot say why. F09, F10 Accept either spelling; answer “this mode earns no attestation” distinctly from “not found”.
Sorry-bearing witnesses are refused in one place and accepted in two. (tester #10) /proposals/witness refuses; /proposals/speculative and /proposals/variant accept, and admission catches it later. F08 (proposals) The same cheap check in all three saves a pull request and a sandbox run.
Builds are accepted against a listed root. (tester #15) Prechecks and submissions on erdos-376 all succeed while claims are refused. This may well be intended — claims are advisory and artifacts during the comment period are worth having — but the two answers disagree and nothing says which is deliberate. D-33 reading; then F05/F07 Decide, then make the site say it: “not claimable, open to artifacts” is a perfectly good state if it is the intended one.
The docs page contradicts the running service. (tester #16) It says no licence text and no DCO text are committed yet, while every write requires accepting the DCO the service serves and every annex requires an SPDX licence. F04 (docs page) Stale copy.
Explainers are advertised as appendable and have no endpoint. (tester #17) The permitted-paths table lists them as writable by anyone; there is no route and no tool, and on the git path classify correctly refuses one on an unproved node — a rule the guide never states. F10 (the guide), F07 if the route is wanted State the rule; decide whether the route exists.
The site cannot show live claims. (tester #23) The frontier page filters the static table while the service's overlay had 21 active claims. This is D-36 working as designed, but the page could at least link the live document. F04 Cosmetic, but it is the first thing a claimer looks for after claiming.
JSON-RPC batches answer with a raw validator dump. (tester #20) Four pydantic validation errors and a docs URL. F09 One structured refusal, like every other tool error.

5. What a contributor to 376 asked for and could not find

The tester's feature list, in its order of usefulness to someone actually trying to make progress on the problem. The first is the one this session would take before any of the defects above except §1.

Show the intake's prior art and attack routes.F04, F11 targets/erdos-376/target.yaml records that the problem is open since 1975, that Erdős, Graham, Ruzsa and Straus proved the two-prime case, that AlphaProof Nexus attempted it in February 2026, and that the curator's route is Kummer's theorem on carries in bases 3, 5 and 7. 6. What worked

Recorded because a register of defects is not a verdict on the system, and two of these were load-bearing.

  • Every refusal the tester probed for was correct. Bad enums, spoofed node and contributor fields (overwritten by the service), oversized bodies, .. path segments, two-node diffs, touching Statement.lean, another identity's precheck job, an anonymous job used for an authenticated submission, a partial label without a relation proof, a related label with one, and a trivial skeleton — all named the field and the rule.
  • All 23 pull requests classified as intended under the pinned gate: nine append, nine proposal each naming its admission directory, four partial, one proof. The D-23 author/committer split held on every push; every submission body carried its attestation block.
  • Appends update the record and the site exactly as designed. Replaying the annex, the postmortem and the approach record: annex_present, attempts and the failure-class histogram all moved on the frontier entry, the node page rendered the annex escaped and labelled untrusted, and the target page listed the approach record.
  • The one thing the protocol most needs to be true was true. The frontier of 376 moved by zero nodes, and correctly so: the root is listed, no claim can be held, and nothing reaches the record without a merge. A fresh agent with a token could not manufacture progress.
a caveat that belongs on every number above: the bench had no Lean and no Docker, and its precheck passed everything instantly. Nothing here is evidence about any contributor's Lean, the tester's included — its own estimates of what would elaborate are in its report, and its annex's numerical aside is wrong (this session recomputed the members below 25000; the Kummer reduction agrees with the direct computation on every one). The two annexes should not be merged as they stand.
