F12 — Statement QA, provenance and drift

D-9 v3.12's mechanizable pass, built: the soundness screens and their kernel-checked exhibits, the grounded review brief, back-translation, equivalence. Plus the record that says what was checked and who signed, the ledger of documented attempts, and the watcher that notices when an inherited statement moves under us or its problem is solved somewhere else.

1. Summary

F11 gives a target a fidelity grade and a place to keep certificates. It does not check anything: a curator asserts a grade and the record takes their word. F12 is the checking. It builds D-9 v3.12's ordered pass as commands that each produce an artifact, keeps a per-statement QA record of what every check found, and refuses to raise a subject to screened-and-signed until that record is complete for the statement as it stands now. It then keeps the record honest over time: the registries the network inherits from correct roughly one statement in ten, and a problem can be resolved elsewhere while the frontier works on it, so D-10 v3.12's watcher runs on a schedule and freezes compute rather than guessing. The two rules that no kernel can carry — that a derived falsehood might be a refutation rather than a defect, and that a related variant's relevance is a human claim — are routed to a person by construction. Cites: D-4, D-8, D-9, D-10, D-11, D-12, D-15, D-16, D-30, D-33, D-34, D-35.

2. User stories

  • As a curator, I want one command per check and a record I cannot forge by hand, so a grade is a summary of evidence rather than an opinion I typed.
  • As a reviewer, I want the kernel-checked exhibits kept apart from the model's opinion, so I can see at a glance which parts of a certificate a machine actually proved.
  • As Mike, doing the one human step, I want the back-translated English beside the source wording and nothing else to read, so signing takes a few minutes and needs no Lean.
  • As a prover, I want compute frozen on a target whose statement moved upstream, so I do not spend a week on a statement that has been corrected.
  • As a bystander, I want the target page to show what was checked, who signed and how many, so "verified" is a link to evidence rather than a word.

3. Scope

In: the qa/v1 record and its exhibit store; opn-gate qa with the screen, brief, back-translate and equivalence subcommands, running in the gate's step-3 sandbox against the graph's pin; defect-claim routing for a positive screen; the attempts ledger and the M count with its statement-hash guard; the fidelity gate on raising a subject to screened-and-signed; the upstream-drift and resolved-elsewhere watchers; the related-variant relevance signature (D-30 v3.12); the QA summary in F03's products and on the site.

Non-goals:

  • Assigning grades. A grade is still a curator act on a certificate (F11); F12 only refuses the ones the record does not support.
  • Canary seeding and per-class catch rates (D-11) — a separate feature; F12's defect classes are D-16's taxonomy unchanged.
  • Adjudicating defect claims (D-15, D-16) — F08 owns the queue; F12 files into it.
  • Upstreaming a correction to Formal Conjectures (D-10) — a documented curator act, not tooled.
  • Deciding which targets to import, and the survey behind that decision. That is F11-T5 and docs/seed_conjecture_sources.html.

4. Requirements

EARS notation, stable IDs.

IDRequirement (EARS)
F12-R1The system shall keep a QA record per subject as targets/<id>/qa/<subject>-<n>.yaml (qa/v1): subject, statement hash, pinned toolchain and Mathlib SHA, and a list of check results {check ∈ {compile, screen-statement, screen-negation, screen-false, screen-consequence, brief, backtranslation, equivalence}, kind ∈ {exhibit, brief}, tool and version, model and version where a model ran, verdict ∈ {pass, fail, inconclusive}, exhibit path or null, timestamp}; append-only, one file per run, exhibits under targets/<id>/qa/exhibits/.
F12-R2The system shall record every check as exactly one of two kinds and shall permit only kind: exhibit to be cited as evidence for a fidelity grade; a kind: brief result shall never raise a grade on its own (D-9 v3.12: layers 2 and 4 emit exhibits, layer 3 emits a judgement).
F12-R3When opn-gate qa screen <target> <subject> runs, the system shall attempt, under a bounded tactic budget and inside the gate's step-3 sandbox on the graph's pinned toolchain: the statement, its negation, False from its hypotheses, and each declared consequence lemma; it shall introduce the subject as an explicit hypothesis or axiom in a scratch file and never as a sorry in a graph file; each success shall write a kernel-checked exhibit and the command shall exit non-zero.
F12-R4When a screen succeeds, the system shall open a defect claim (F08) carrying the exhibit with its class unset, and shall not itself label the finding a misformalization or a refutation; the claim shall name both readings and require a curator to route it (D-9 v3.12, D-8, D-12).
F12-R5When the statement version changes or the graph's Mathlib pin moves, the system shall mark every QA record for that subject stale and re-run the screens, because junk-value defects appear and disappear with the library beneath the statement (D-9 v3.12).
F12-R6When opn-gate qa brief runs, the system shall produce a review brief listing every constant the statement references with its definition as elaborated under the pin, plus the structural type signature, recorded with kind: brief.
F12-R7When opn-gate qa backtranslate runs, the system shall render the statement into English from the Lean alone with the informal source withheld from the model, write it beside the source wording for comparison, and record the model and version; if the subject's provenance names an AI formalizer, the command shall refuse a back-translating model of the same family and shall record formalizer: unknown where provenance does not say.
F12-R8When opn-gate qa equivalence <a> <b> runs, the system shall attempt both implications between two formalizations; a proved pair shall be recorded as an exhibit, and a failure shall be recorded inconclusive and never as negative evidence (D-9 v3.12).
F12-R9The system shall refuse opn-gate fidelity raising a subject to screened-and-signed unless the QA record for the current statement hash shows the pass complete with no unrouted positive screen, and the attestor differs from the subject's author (D-9).
F12-R10The system shall keep documented external attempts as targets/<id>/attempts.yaml {venue, system, date, url, statement hash} and shall count toward D-9's M only entries whose statement hash equals the subject's current hash; on a D-8 revision the count shall reset and the record shall show the attempts that no longer apply rather than deleting them.
F12-R11The system shall run a scheduled watcher that, for each target whose provenance names an upstream path and commit, diffs that path at the pinned commit against upstream head; if they differ, then it shall open a revision request carrying the diff, set the target's drift flag, and freeze proving compute by deriving claimable: false, leaving the fidelity grade unchanged (D-10 v3.12).
F12-R12The same watcher shall check each source's problem status and, on a flip to resolved, shall flag the target for D-33 dormancy with the citation attached and notify the curator; it shall never change a target's status by itself.
F12-R13The system shall require a variant whose relation is related to carry relevance {text ≤ 500 chars, signer identity, date} before products mark it published as pertinent to the target; resolves and partial variants shall require nothing beyond their relation proof (D-30 v3.12).
F12-R14F03's products and the site shall carry, per target: the QA pass state per check, the signature count with the named signers, the counted attempts, and the drift flag with its date; untrusted upstream text in a diff or a brief shall be escaped (conventions §4).

6. Budgets / limits

  • Screen tactic budget: 60 s per attempt, 5 min per subject, inside the sandbox. Provisional until measured on the on-ramp graph (Q4).
  • Review brief: 200 KB, definitions elaborated one level deep.
  • Watcher: daily, one upstream fetch per distinct source repository, never per target.
  • Evidence text on a certificate stays F11's 2,000 characters; exhibits are files, not text.

7. Security & privacy notes

  • The screens elaborate Lean that is trying to prove False. They run in the gate's step-3 sandbox on the pinned toolchain, never on the host and never in the api process (conventions §4, constitution C9).
  • The brief and the back-translation send statement text to a model provider. Statements are public by construction, so nothing secret leaves; the provider key is a C8 secret and lives where the other service secrets do.
  • Upstream diffs, briefs and back-translations are untrusted text: capped, escaped at render, never interpolated into a command.
  • The watcher reads public repositories unauthenticated where it can, so a token outage degrades it to a warning rather than a false all-clear.

8. Dependencies

  • F11 (required): the target record, the fidelity command and certificates, the sources and provenance fields the watcher reads, and the screened-and-signed schema bump.
  • F08 (required): defect claims and revision requests — F12 files into that queue and never adjudicates.
  • F03, F04: products and the site surface.
  • Protocol: written against decisions v3.12 (D-9's pass and rung, D-10's watched pin, D-30's relevance signature).
  • Packages: one model client for the brief and back-translation, pinned and locked, with its C5 justification recorded at T3. No new Lean dependency: the screens use the graph's pinned Mathlib and the gate's existing sandbox.

9. Open questions / judgment calls

IDCall made — for review
F12-Q1The watcher runs in this repo, not the graph. D-35 keeps the graph's history mathematics only, and a daily drift check is tooling. It opens a revision request against the graph the same way any contribution does. The alternative, a scheduled workflow in the graph repo, would put service commits in a history that must stay readable.
F12-Q2The attempts ledger lives in the graph, the survey stays here. An attempt is evidence for a grade on a specific statement, so it belongs beside the certificate; the catalog of candidates the network passed over is research and stays in docs/ (decided with Mike, 2026-09-09).
F12-Q3A failed equivalence writes nothing negative (R8). The 2026 evidence is that the check is sound in one direction only: a proved pair is strong, a failure is uninformative. The risk accepted is that real drift can hide behind an inconclusive, which is why equivalence is a bonus layer and not part of the floor.
F12-Q4The tactic budgets are guesses until the on-ramp graph measures them (§6). Too low and the screens miss a shallow refutation; too high and every pin move costs an hour of CI. T2 records the measured distribution and the numbers move once, with evidence.
F12-Q5Model choice is recorded, not fixed by this spec. R7's independence rule needs a family name per model, so the record carries model and version and the refusal is by family string; a provider swap is then a config change and a new row in the record, not a spec change.
F12-Q6A positive screen is never auto-labelled (R4), which means the defect queue can carry a finding that is actually a refutation of an open conjecture. That is the correct place for it: routing is cheap and mislabelling a refutation as a defect, or the reverse, is not.
