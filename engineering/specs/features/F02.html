F02 — Hazard checkers

Gate step 6 (D-4): static checkers over the statement for the ways a Lean theorem can be true and meaningless, plus the native_decide waiver path D-4 names.

1. Summary

Step 6 runs six named checkers over a node's statement — ℕ subtraction, division by zero, junk values, Int truncation, unused binders, off-by-one ranges — and fails on any finding the node's author has not explicitly acknowledged. Because statements are immutable (D-8) and admitted mechanically (D-29), the checkers bite at admission; at proof time they re-run and, by construction, repeat the admission result — the re-run exists so the authoritative gate never trusts a prior verdict (D-4: fail-closed, in order). F02 also completes step 5: a proof using native_decide passes only with a recorded justification, a reviewer sign-off that names it, and a permanent flag on the attestation. Cites: D-3 (META.yaml), D-4 step 6 and the native_decide amendment, D-8, D-15, D-29, D-34.

2. User stories

  • As a statement reviewer, I want the mechanical hazards found before I look (D-15: mechanical checkers run first), so my time goes to what only a human can see.
  • As a node proposer, I want to acknowledge a flagged construct with a one-line reason when it is intentional, so a legitimate ℕ subtraction does not block a real lemma.
  • As a bystander, I want a node proved with native_decide to say so permanently, so I know its trust base is the compiler.

3. Scope

In: the checker framework in gate/lean/ with the six checkers as one executable; per-graph enablement via gate-spec.json; step 6 in the pipeline; acknowledgment via META.yaml (schema meta/v2); the standalone opn-gate hazards command F08's admission will call; the native_decide waiver path; fixtures for every hazard and both test tiers.

Non-goals:

  • Admission of new nodes end to end — F08.
  • Reviewer assignment and canaries (D-11, D-15) — Stage 1.
  • Rendering the compiler-trust flag on the site — F04 reads it from the attestation.

4. Requirements

IDRequirement (EARS)
F02-R1The system shall provide the executable opn-hazards in gate/lean/ that runs the checkers named on its command line over the theorem in a Statement.lean and prints findings as JSON: {checker, location, message}.
F02-R2The system shall implement checkers with ids nat-sub (subtraction on ℕ in the statement), div-zero (division or modulo whose divisor is not syntactically non-zero), junk-value (a total function applied outside its meaningful domain: Nat.pred 0, List.head!-style defaults, Real.sqrt of a possibly negative argument), int-trunc (a coercion or division on ℤ/ℕ that truncates), unused-binder (a universally quantified variable nothing depends on), off-by-one-range (a Finset.range, Ico/Icc or ≤/< boundary adjacent to an explicit literal).
F02-R3When step 6 runs, the system shall run exactly the checkers listed in gate-spec.json hazard_checkers; if that list names an unknown id, then the gate shall fail before step 1 with a configuration diagnostic.
F02-R4When step 6 produces a finding that is not acknowledged in META.yaml acknowledged_hazards — an entry matching on checker and location with a non-empty justification — then step 6 shall fail naming every unacknowledged finding.
F02-R5When step 6 passes with acknowledgments used, the verdict shall list them so a reviewer sees what was waved through.
F02-R6The system shall bump the META schema to meta/v2 adding optional acknowledged_hazards; meta/v1 remains readable and equivalent to an empty list (D-34 versioning).
F02-R7The system shall expose opn-gate hazards <node-dir> that runs step 6 alone and exits 0 or 1 with the same JSON, for use by admission (F08) and by proposers locally.
F02-R8When step 5 detects native_decide (F00-R6), the system shall pass the step only if the submission includes nodes/<id>/waivers/native_decide.yaml validating against waiver/v1 (justification, author); waivers/ becomes a permitted path for that node only when the proof uses native_decide.
F02-R9When a waived proof merges, the post-merge job shall require the approving review body to contain the line waiver: native_decide, shall otherwise refuse to merge, and on merge shall write trust_base: compiler into the attestation; the attestation schema gains that optional field as attestation/v2.
F02-R10Step 6 shall be deterministic: two runs over the same statement and checker list produce byte-identical findings, and a proof-time run reproduces the admission-time findings.

6. Budgets / limits

  • opn-hazards under 30 s per statement on the fixtures; findings capped at 200 per statement.

7. Security & privacy notes

  • Checkers elaborate the statement, which is admitted content, but they still run only in the sandbox or locally (C9).
  • Acknowledgment justifications and waiver justifications are contributor free text: short, typed, served as untrusted data downstream (D-28).

8. Dependencies

  • F00, F01 (required): pipeline, seam, Lake package.
  • Packages: none new.

9. Open questions / judgment calls

IDCall made — for review
F02-Q1Hazards are admission-time failures with an acknowledgment escape — decided in D-4 and D-29 (v3.11). The acknowledgment lives in META.yaml as acknowledged_hazards; the reviewer sees it (D-15). No deviation.
F02-Q2Waiver mechanics — decided in D-4 (v3.11): justification as a file in the submission, sign-off as a line in the approval, flag on the attestation projected into graph.json and the site, never an edit to META.yaml. No deviation.
F02-Q3Checker precision is deliberately conservative. Each checker flags syntactic patterns; false positives are cheap (one acknowledgment line), false negatives are what D-11's canaries measure later. Definitions in R2 are the v1 contract; tightening is a checker version, recorded in gate-spec.json by id@version.
F02-Q4A finding's location is the offending subterm, pretty-printed in its binder context (n - 1, a / b), not a line:column (decided at T1, 2026-09-09). The checkers run over the elaborated statement type, where source positions are gone but the term is exact, and the subterm is what a proposer writes into acknowledged_hazards and what a reviewer reads; it survives re-indentation of the file, which a position would not. Two identical subterms yield one finding (deduplicated), so one acknowledgment covers both. Findings are sorted by (checker, location, message) for R10.
F02-Q5Waiver mechanics as built (T4, 2026-09-09). (a) R9 names attestation/v2, but v2 was published on 2026-09-08 for the step-9 review block and D-34 forbids editing it, so trust_base lands in attestation/v3; v1–v3 stay readable by the bounce rule and the signer. (b) trust_base is written by every run from the checked tree (compiler iff step 5 accepted a waiver), not only by the post-merge job, so a D-5 reproduction matches the committed record; the post-merge job is where it becomes permanent, and it refuses to attest (exit 1, nothing written) unless an approving review body contains the line waiver: native_decide (--approval-body-file; gate.yml must pass the approving reviews' bodies — T5's graph-repo diff). (c) Step 2 cannot see axioms, so it admits waivers/native_decide.yaml only when Proof.lean textually mentions native_decide (AC8); step 5 is the authority: it fails native-decide-unwaived without the file, waiver-invalid when it does not validate, and waiver-unneeded when the file is present but the proof rests on the kernel. Under a waiver the native_decide axioms are exempt from the allowlist and every other axiom is still checked.
F02-Q6A run killed for memory is not a timeout (T6, 2026-09-20). R12 turns a container killed by the cap into the TimeoutExpired every step reports as timeout, and reads "killed by the cap" as exit 124 or 137. But 137 is any SIGKILL: timeout -s KILL sends one, and so does the kernel's OOM killer when the container reaches --memory. Graph PR #123 (the 2026-09-19 primes run; run 35491033780) reported step 4 exceeded the 600s wall-clock cap twenty-eight seconds after the olean cache hit that begins step 4, on a proof whose precheck had passed an hour earlier. The clock cannot have done that, so the likeliest reading is a memory kill — an inference: the run kept no record that would say, which is the defect. Docker does record it, as State.OOMKilled. Decided: on exit 137 the sandbox asks, and a memory kill raises MemoryExceeded naming memory_mib; step 4, where the builds that can do this run, reports memory-exceeded, "killed at the N MiB memory cap, not by the clock". MemoryExceeded is a TimeoutExpired on purpose: nineteen other call sites already fail a killed run safely through that except, and they go on doing so until one has a reason to name the cause (C7). Exit 124 is timeout's own code and stays the clock's. Not decided here: why that proof should need the cap after its holes were proved and not before — F07-T21 stages a proved hole's module into the parent's build, and the precheck that passed predates the holes; to be reproduced once the diagnostic says which cap it was. Live at the next re-pin (D-35). Reversal: the one check.
F02-Q7Step 4 replays one module per process (T7, 2026-09-20; D-4 v3.16 unchanged). T6 made a memory kill say so, and within hours it did: agent E's three prechecks of a three-line proof each answered memory-exceeded, "killed at the 4096 MiB memory cap, not by the clock", on leanchecker Nodes Defs, deterministically. Read against the record, it is not one proof's problem. Every passing attestation on a Mathlib target whose node has dependencies is either from a gate older than v3.16's replay (#19, #38) or a partial, whose holes are unproved and so never staged; two nodes with a proved dependency have tried, five attempts in all (graph PR #123 twice under the old "timeout" label, agent E three times), and every one was killed. Since v3.16 no proof with a proved dependency had passed step 4 on a Mathlib target: a skeleton could go in, its holes be proved, and the parent never closed. Cause: the Mathlib-pinned replay ran one leanchecker over the graph's prefixes, so one process held every staged module's Mathlib-sized environment at once; alone, a node's own proof fits under the cap. The owner chose the fix over raising the cap (which treats the symptom and widens what hostile Lean may use): replay_plan names the modules the graph built, not their prefixes, and replay runs one leanchecker per module, in sequence, stopping at the first that fails. What is replayed is exactly what v3.16 says — the node, every dependency and every compiled Defs module — so the decision is untouched. The modules share the step's one wall-clock budget, each call given what is left, and running out is the step's timeout. The step now goes through the same replay function the QA passes use, so there is one seam. Real toolchain: leanchecker takes an exact module name without --fresh as it takes a prefix, the whole plan passes run that way, and a name with no olean is refused. Not yet shown: the memory itself, which needs the Mathlib image under the cap; it is shown on the live graph by re-running agent E's precheck after the re-pin, and the ledger says so until then. Reversal: the loop in replay.
F02-Q8A function type is not an unused binder (T8, 2026-09-21). An outside agent's precheck of a hole reading ∃ a b : ℕ → ℤ, … drew, at step 6, "binder a._@._internal._hyg.0 is quantified but nothing after it mentions it". ℕ → ℤ is a ∀ in the kernel whose anonymous binder nothing mentions, and unused-binder reported it as a quantified variable. On a gate-written hole the finding is recorded and not refused (F07-Q19), which is why it did no harm; on a proposed statement it would have refused a correct submission until acknowledged. Only a ∀ that is a proposition is reported now. Reproduced with the real toolchain first, in the agent's own words; the fixture's genuine unused m still fires. Reversal: one line.
