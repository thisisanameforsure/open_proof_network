F31 — Shelving

A steward of the target or a listed curator takes a statement, or one merged decomposition, off the root's path from the website: a signed shelving record with a cause, derived (never written) into every consumer — off the frontier, work refused, drawn only on request — and lifted by one later record (decisions v3.47, the owner's reading of erdos-257's route 2 on 2026-10-24).

1. Summary

On 2026-10-24 the owner read erdos-257's problem page and found route 2 (the t1023-f1 partial of 2026-10-23: fourteen proved special-case variants, the open hole erdos-257--h18 and the Tao–Teräväinen leaf) a distraction that does not help the root, with no act on the site to remove it and nothing to keep agents off its open holes. The record had three mechanisms and none fit: D-14 abandoned is a verdict that a branch is dead, curator-only, written by command line and pull request, and an abandoned statement is still drawn; a merged partial cannot be withdrawn; and stewards have no removal act at all.

This feature adds one record kind and one route. A shelving/v1 record under a node, signed by the approval key for a signed-in steward of the target or a curator (or SSH-signed by hand), says shelved or unshelved, with scope node (the statement and every hole child beneath it) or route (one merged decomposition, named by its file, and its holes) and a cause. Shelving is not a status: a proved statement stays proved. The products derive shelved on each graph.json row and in the bundle; the frontier omits shelved statements; the gate and the service refuse work on one with node-shelved; the sufficient sets drop every set resting on a shelved unproved statement; and the site hides shelved statements from the routes view, keeps them under the existing toggle, and gives stewards and curators the Shelve, Unshelve and Retire-route controls.

The owner's rulings, taken in the planning conversation (Q1–Q6): one signer, no second review; proved variants hide too, behind the toggle; reach stops at the root, a hole another live route still needs, and a statement a merged proof uses, and never climbs; the name is shelved, lifted by unshelved; a shared hole stays available under a node-scope cascade as under a retirement; a shelved proved statement keeps its routes live.

2. User stories

  • As a steward reading my problem's page, I want to take a decomposition that does not serve the root off the drawing and off the frontier in one act from the site, with my reasoning published, so that the page shows the proof and agents stop spending work there.
  • As a curator, I want the same act on any target, and a way to reverse it by one record.
  • As an agent, I want a shelved statement to be refused before I spend a precheck on it, with the cause in the refusal and in the bundle, and never to see it on the frontier.
  • As a reader, I want to be able to see what was shelved and why, on request, with nothing deleted.

3. Scope

In: gate/schemas/shelving/v1.json, the next graph/ and context/ versions; gate/opn_gate/shelving.py (record, signature, derivation), the role in paths and modes, the pre-merge refusal, routes and closing, products and context, the curator CLI; the service route POST /shelvings and the refusals in precheck, submissions, claims, proposals and the fast check; the MCP get_node surface through the bundle; the site's drawing, node page, routes list, key and Docs page; the guide. Out (non-goals): an MCP write tool (a person's act, as the panel's); any change to status or its enums; any ranking of the frontier (D-25); shelving a target; un-merging a partial.

F31-R9The Docs state page shall name the act, the guide shall say what shelved means and that work on it is refused, and opn-gate shelve shall write an SSH-signed record for the hand path.

6. Budgets / limits

A cause is 1–1000 characters. Rate limit: the approvals bucket per login per hour. No lean or docker tier is touched: the derivation, the refusals and the route are fast-tier work.

7. Security & privacy notes

Every record is signed; the service signs with the approval key only for a login the session proves and the roles the record names (C8). A cause is a person's words and is demarcated where shown (D-31). Nothing is deleted; one commit's revert restores everything.

8. Dependencies

F18 (uses), F23 (web session, approval key, literature confirm as the pattern), F27/F30 (routes, sets, the one drawing), F28 (closing links).

9. Open questions / judgment calls

F31-Q1 — One signer (owner, 2026-10-24). An active steward of the target or a listed curator, with no second review, as the literature confirmation works.

F31-Q2 — Proved variants hide too (owner, 2026-10-24). The page is for following the proof; shelved proved statements leave the routes view and stay under the toggle.

F31-Q3 — Reach (owner, 2026-10-24). A node-scope shelving reaches the node and its hole children; a retirement reaches the decomposition's holes and theirs; neither reaches the root, a hole another live route of an unshelved parent still needs, or a statement a merged proof uses; a retirement never climbs, and a statement joined to the root by its own relation is shelved separately.

F31-Q4 — Name (owner, 2026-10-24). shelved, lifted by unshelved: a judgment, not a verdict.

F31-Q5 — A shared hole stays (owner, 2026-10-24, on the design review's finding). Live case: erdos-1094--h3@2 uses erdos-1094--h2--h1--h1 as a lemma; shelving erdos-1094--h2 must not take it off the frontier. The exception applies to both scopes.

F31-Q6 — A shelved proved statement keeps its routes (owner, 2026-10-24). A kernel-checked proof is a fact; Route.live is false only when retired or when a hole is shelved and unproved; such a member is drawn dim in a set. Route 2 of erdos-257 dies regardless.

F31-Q7 — Not a status, and its own record kind. A service-opened node-status record is refused as a curator act, node-status/v1 has no signature fields, and latest-wins over that directory would let an unshelved record hide a superseded or stale one. So shelving has its own directory and schema, and graph.json gains a field beside status rather than a value in it (the lesson of speculative, 2026-09-19: a label is not a verdict). Frontier and targets-index schemas are unchanged because shelved nodes are simply absent.

F31-Q8 — Arrivals, not prefixes. Inheritance is computed top-down over arrivals in dependency order, never as a fixed point, and never from the --h id prefix (a revision folds the old node's holes onto -v2); a same-statement revision inherits the parent's shelving along the chain, and a route record's partial is matched by path, holder-agnostic. A record on a used node is void at derivation if a later proof uses it. Two sources: the gate derives from the parent tree, the service reads the row and accepts the render window, as literature does.

F31-Q9 — Unshelve is an action. A withdrawal is a curator role and names only status/, defects/, gloss/ and explainer/; an unshelve carries its own cause and date. A curator withdrawal of a shelving record is still honoured, so "absent" has one rule.

F31-Q10 — Rule 4 is latest per key (gate, service). A node's standing is its latest scope: node record; each route's retirement is the latest record naming that route. Retiring route 2 does not lift route 1, and an unshelved record lifts the key it names (scope: route with the route, or the node). The service's "changes nothing" check uses the same key: already-shelved when the row says the node is shelved by its own record, or every hole of the named route is retired through this node; not-shelved only when the row carries no shelving at all, so an unshelve on a node shelved by inheritance is allowed (it is how a child is exempted, Q3). node-used is refused for action: shelved only.

F31-Q11 — via is the record's home. For an inherited shelving, via is the node whose shelving/ directory holds the record, which is what makes record resolvable; a same-statement revision that inherits its predecessor's record names the predecessor.

F31-Q12 — A retirement is read off the holes. graph/v15 publishes shelved on rows, not on decompositions[] (a closed object), so the routes, the closing links and the site read a route as retired when its holes carry a route-scope shelving with via the route's node. Exact except for a retired route all of whose holes another live route still needs, which reads live and closes nothing new. A retired field on decompositions[] in the next graph version would make it exact; left for a later contract. Route.retired and Hole.shelved are not in the MCP's list_sufficient_sets answer (its v4 schema is closed), so the "abandoned or superseded" wording and the new fields wait for a v5 of that result schema; live carries the effect today.

F31-Q13 — Refusal placement. Gate: shelved_refusal for proof, partial, alternate, witness completion and a proposed-for pointer, from the parent tree with the pull request's own shelving records excluded. Service: precheck.check_open (precheck and submissions, ahead of blocked and superseded), claims.off_frontier, hole_awaiting_witness, proposed_for_node; POST /check warns with the whole shelved object. A proposal whose deps name a shelved statement is not refused: a shelved proved statement is still a fact (Q6). A dependency on a shelved unproved statement is left unrefused too, for the owner to rule on.

F31-Q14 — Codes. No bad-field: the service's body checks answer arguments-invalid naming the field, as every F23/F05 route does. An unsigned record is record-invalid (the schema requires key and signature), so shelving-signature means a tampered record or an approval-key record under another key; shelving-node mirrors literature-node. node-shelved, node-root, node-used and shelving-route-unknown are emitted by the gate and the service alike (source both). The duplicate rule's written key is node action scope route, cause excluded.

F31-Q15 — The products do not re-verify a shelving record's signature at render. shelving.load validates the schema only, where literature.derive re-verifies. The gate is the guard; if a record that reached the tree by another route should count for nothing, it is one signature_problem call in load with a signer and the approval key passed through load_target. The owner's call.

F31-Q16 — Q9 is live only with a withdrawal/v3. withdrawal/v2's withdraws pattern admits status/, defects/, gloss/ and explainer/ only; shelving.load already honours records.withdrawn_names(node_dir, "shelving"), so a v3 adding the prefix (D-34: a new version) makes a curator withdrawal of a shelving record read as absent with no code change.

F31-Q17 — The site's words. A shelved unproved statement reads "shelved" (render.SHELVED, returned by node_state in place of open / needs-witness / blocked, so the Problems page's workable set still equals the frontier's claimable set); a shelved proved statement keeps "proved"; the node head reads "open · shelved". "shelved" is a route-mark key, not a status, so it is in neither LEGEND_EXTRA nor the Docs state map. Retire is offered on every drawn route not already cut; the root's page carries no shelve slot; the routes list is a new sibling after the sets table (.routes-list-wrap), an addition to the F30 contract note, which graph.js never reads.

F31-Q18 — The root's routes are retired like any other's, from the sets table (the owner, 2026-10-25). The first live act was refused node-root: a retirement sits on the node whose decomposition it names, and route 2 of erdos-257 is the root's. Both the service's write-time rule and the gate's pre-merge rule were keyed on the node alone; they are keyed on scope: node now, since a retirement never shelves the node it sits on (R2) and the root's own decompositions are what a steward most wants to retire. The derivation needed no change: a route-scope record on the root cuts the arrival into each of its holes and the root stays. The gate half binds the graph only at the next re-pin. And the owner read the separate routes list as confusing: the retire control lives in the sufficient-sets table now, a Routes column naming each set's routes (span.set-route[data-route], approach as the title) with the hidden .retire-act beside each, so a route a set goes through is retired on the row that shows why it matters; a route no live set rests on is in no row, and its holes are shelved from their own pages.
