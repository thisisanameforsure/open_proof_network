F26 — The formalization review

A conjecture that arrives in words becomes Lean through a review, not a curator's single statement: candidates from people and models, each filtered by kernel-checked exhibits and intent tests, reconciled in pairs, questioned in mathematics to the one person who knows what was meant, mutated to measure the tests, and signed by two named roles on an evidence page. Existing targets keep their grade and get a review in the background; a proof that settles a root merges only when its review is complete, unless a reliable source already vouches for the statement.

1. Summary

Decisions v3.39 applies the amendment drafted on 2026-10-09 as "v3.36" and settled by the owner on 2026-10-10 (engineering/session-notes/2026-10-09-amendment-v3.36-formalization-rounds.md, §1–§8; prototyped twice, §9 and §10). It is numbered v3.39 because v3.37 (Palomar) and v3.38 (lemmas) were applied first; the draft's number stays in its file name. The amendment rewrites D-9's pass as a formalization review with seven stages, adds intent tests with three probes and a format rule, records equivalence pairs per direction with refutations kept, names two signers by role and keeps the grade ladder earned under the review, makes a reading not chosen a D-30 variant, removes back-translation from the pass, and puts the evidence on one page where the proposer answers and the GitHub App tells them. Who may open a review is a policy.json setting.

The evidence base is the two prototypes: 14 of 14 planted defects caught on core Lean with one cluster surviving per conjecture (engineering/evidence/v3.36-prototype/), and 13 of 13 on three live Mathlib targets through the network's own fast check, with a real error from a blind agent caught and the incumbent statements passing their own review (engineering/evidence/v3.36-prototype-mathlib/). Their code (engineering/prototypes/formalization-round/, …-review-mathlib/) is the reference the gate's review job is rebuilt from; nothing of it is imported as is.

2. User stories

  • As the mathematician who proposed a problem, I answer questions about what I meant, in mathematics, on one page, and I am told on GitHub when a question is waiting; I never read Lean unless I want to.
  • As an agent, I submit a candidate formalization of a new problem from the words alone, I see why a candidate was discarded as a kernel-checked exhibit, and I find the open pair directions as claimable work.
  • As a person who formalizes, I submit my candidate blind, before any other candidate's Lean is shown to me, and the record says my version is a person's.
  • As an independent signer, I read the surviving cluster's Lean rendered mechanically beside its gloss, the test matrix, the pairs and the questions, and I sign that the evidence supports it, or send it back with a question.
  • As a prover on a target that exists already, nothing changes until I prove the root: then the merge waits for the review to be complete, or for the catalog evidence that already waives step 9.
  • As a curator, I open a review on a target that has none, change who may open one by one visible pull request, and see every review's state beside its grade.

3. Scope

In: the amendment applied (D-6, D-9, D-28, D-30, D-35, D-36, glossary); the review's records on the graph, append-only and derived, never rewritten; the gate's review job (stages 3, 4 and 6) running in the step-3 sandbox and writing exhibits as bot commits; intent tests with the three probes; the pair records and open directions as claimable work; questions, answers and signatures as records; step 9 reading the review; the policy setting; the service routes and MCP tools; the App's notice; the evidence page and the proposer's answer form on the site; the review state in the products; a background review opened on every existing target; the first live review on a new intake.

Out: any automated verdict on intent (every signature is a person's decision, D-9 v3.12); a model-written expected value for a test (R6); back-translation anywhere in the pass (R10); an email address held by the network (R15); a review on definitions (D-9's second review object keeps the QA pass of F12 for now, Q4); paying for candidates (D-19 credits them as statement lines); any change to how variants are proved (D-30's labels are unchanged; R11 only adds a way variants arise).

4. Requirements

IDRequirement (EARS)
F26-R1Records. A review shall live under targets/<t>/review/ as append-only records (D-3): review/v1 (review.yaml: the opening, who opened it in which role, the day, the words and the proposer's cases); candidate/v1 (candidates/<id>.yaml with its <id>.lean: author, the models that helped or none, whether its author declares having read another candidate's Lean, and for a ported statement its source); intent-test/v1 (tests/<id>.yaml and .lean: the case, the probe among holds, covers, witness, the expected result and its source among answer, citation, agreement); review-question/v1 (questions/<id>.yaml: the question in words and what raised it); review-answer/v1 (answers/<id>.yaml: the intent signer's answer, its cited source or none, signed by the approval key naming the login); pair/v1 (pairs/<a>--<b>.yaml: each direction proved, refuted or open with its exhibit or its attempt); exhibit/v1 (exhibits/<id>.json and .lean: a kernel-checked result the gate wrote); and review-signature/v1 (signatures/<role>-<date>.yaml: intent or independent, with the decision accept, split or send-back). No record shall be edited; a later record supersedes an earlier one by reference, and the review's state shall be derived from the set (F08-T10's principle).
F26-R2Opening. When a target is proposed in words (D-6) or a curator or steward asks, a review shall open if the asker's role is in policy.json's review_openers (policy/v4; default proposer, steward, curator; each change dated with its reason by one curator pull request). For every target that exists at the re-pin, the network shall open a review in the background whatever the setting says, with the live statement as the first candidate credited to its source's authors and wrapped where the format rule needs it (R5), proved equal by Iff.rfl, the live text and hash untouched.
F26-R3Candidates, blind. Anyone with a token shall submit a candidate through the service; a person's candidate is marked as a person's when its models is empty and the submitter's automation method is manual. Until their own candidate is on the record, a person asking the service for a review shall be shown the words, the definitions and the cases, never another candidate's Lean (the service, the MCP and the site all hold to it; the graph itself is public and the declaration of R1 is shown as a claim, not a fact). An agent's candidate shall name its model (v3.31).
F26-R4Filter. The gate's review job shall compile every candidate at the pin, screen it (prove it, prove its negation, prove False from its hypotheses, within D-9's bounded budget), and run every intent test against it three ways (holds, covers, witness). A candidate that fails any shall be marked discarded with the exhibit as its reason and shall stay on the record. A candidate the format rule returns (R5) shall be marked returned, never discarded.
F26-R5Format rule. A candidate shall name each concept the words use as its own predicate (the review's manifest names the concepts); one that writes a concept inline shall be returned to its author with that reason. For a ported statement the review shall add a wrapper naming the parts, proved equal to the original by rfl or Iff.rfl.
F26-R6Intent tests. A model may propose which small, degenerate or boundary cases suit the statement and write them in Lean, or record that small cases do not apply and why. No model shall supply an expected value: each test's expected result shall come from an answer, a citation or the agreement of every surviving candidate, the record naming which, the last shown weakest. Where candidates answer a case differently no test shall be written; the case becomes a question (R7). Where every candidate is vacuous on a case the page shows a triviality flag. Where candidates' predicates for one concept disagree on a value, the value is a question. Tests shall re-run when the statement is revised (D-8) or the Mathlib pin moves.
F26-R7Reconcile and ask. The job shall compare candidates in pairs: concept predicates matched first, then the claims with the concepts as atoms, by the bounded tactics the prototype used; each direction recorded proved, refuted (a kernel-checked instance, never a proof that the statements differ) or open with the attempt. The degenerate guard (a proof that never uses its hypothesis) shall refuse a direction as proof of equivalence. Candidates with both directions proved share a cluster. Each proved disagreement between clusters, each concept disagreement and each surviving mutant (R8) shall become a question in words, phrased as the hypothesis ("Suppose 5 were the only prime. Would the conjecture be satisfied at n = 2?"), put to the intent signer. An answer shall become an intent test and the job shall run again.
F26-R8Open directions are work. An open pair direction shall appear as claimable work on the target's review (the frontier is unchanged, Q6) on any root not yet expert-attested and after it whenever a new candidate arrives; a proof or refutation of a direction shall be submitted through the gate as a pair-proof artifact, kernel-checked, and credited (D-19). Mutate. The job shall perturb the surviving cluster's representative by D-11's defect classes on the named parts; a mutant every test passes becomes a question; the page reports how many mutants the tests caught, as a measure of the tests.
F26-R9Signatures and the ladder. A certificate shall name two signers by role: the intent signer (the proposer; for an inherited conjecture the target's steward, each answer citing a source or flagged) and the independent signer (reads Lean, wrote no candidate in the surviving cluster, is not the intent signer). The ladder is earned as the table in the amendment says: screened-and-signed when the automated stages are complete and one non-author signs; author-attested when the intent signer signs; expert-attested with both and a person-written candidate in the cluster, else capped and shown. "Author" means the author of the Lean everywhere. No grade held today shall be taken away. Each signer chooses accept, split (R11) or send back with a question; the page never computes a signature.
F26-R10Back-translation leaves. qa.py's floors shall drop backtranslation; existing QA records stay valid (they did more than the new floor asks) and the qa schema keeps the value for old records. The signer reads the Lean rendered mechanically with every type and coercion shown, beside the gloss (v3.30).
F26-R11Split. Where the intent signer confirms that surviving clusters are genuinely different readings, the chosen cluster's representative becomes the root and each other becomes a D-30 variant with its label proved where a pair direction already is, else related; never the root, never the certificate. A later choice of another cluster revises the root (D-8) and keeps the old one as a variant.
F26-R12When work opens; step 9. A new target becomes claimable when one cluster survives stage 3 and stage 6 raises no open question. A proof that would settle a target's root merges only when the review is complete (automated stages done, no open question, grade at least screened-and-signed) or v3.15's catalog evidence scores at least five, the evidence recording why the source is reliable; the automated stages still run and never hold that merge. Intermediate work never waits.
F26-R13The job. opn-gate review shall run stages 3, 4 and 6 in the step-3 sandbox from a target's records, write exhibits, pair records and the review state as one bot commit (as the post-merge job writes holes), elaborate every Lean it writes before committing it (2026-09-17), and be idempotent: a second run on the same records writes nothing. The post-merge job shall run it after any merge that adds a review record, and review.yml shall run it on dispatch and nightly for every open review.
F26-R14Service and MCP. POST /reviews (open one; a web session or a bearer with a GitHub login whose role the policy allows), POST /reviews/<t>/candidates (bearer; the blind rule), POST /reviews/<t>/answers (web session; the intent signer only), POST /reviews/<t>/signatures (web session; the role's conditions checked from the record), GET /reviews/<t> and GET /reviews/<t>/questions (open). MCP tools get_review, list_review_questions, submit_candidate and list_open_pairs; a pair proof goes through submit_proof with artifact_type: pair-proof. Every write refuses before any pull request opens (F13-T21's rule) and every refusal has a catalog row.
F26-R15The notice. When a run leaves questions waiting that no notice has named, the App shall post one comment on the review's tracking issue in the graph repository (or the proposal's pull request) @-mentioning the intent signer, listing the questions and linking the page; one comment per batch. The same notice shall appear on the signer's /me/ page. The network holds no email address.
F26-R16The page. The site shall render one review page per target from the record only: candidates with author, origin and stage-3 result; pairs and clusters; questions, answers with sources and the tests they became; the test matrix with mutants caught; screens and the brief marked as a model's judgement; the two signature slots. Exhibits and judgements shall be visibly apart. The intent signer, signed in (v3.33), answers on this page; the signers sign on it. The target page shall show the review's state beside the grade.
F26-R17Products. targets-index and graph.json shall take their next versions with review: state (candidates, clusters, questions waiting, complete), the grade, the signers by role, and the open pair directions; context shall carry the review's words and open questions for an agent on the node.
F26-R18Documentation. The guide shall gain a "Formalization review" section (how to submit a candidate blind, read a discard, claim an open pair, what a question looks like), the Docs page a "How a statement is reviewed" section, the error catalog every new code, and the MCP appendix every new tool; the walkthrough shall execute the new blocks against the fixture and the fake service.

6. Budgets / limits

  • A review's job runs under the step-3 sandbox's caps; each screen, test and pair attempt has D-9's bounded tactic budget (the prototype's 20 s per hosted call becomes the sandbox's budget per attempt), and a run that exceeds its wall clock records what it finished and leaves the rest open.
  • Candidates per review per identity: 3 a day (the write limit applies as well); questions per notice: unbounded, one comment per batch.
  • Mutants: every single-site change of the named parts by D-11's classes, capped at 200 per representative; the cap is recorded when hit.
  • The page: one per target, rendered from the products; a review with more than 50 candidates folds the discarded ones.

7. Security & privacy notes

  • Only exhibits are evidence for a grade (D-9 v3.12); the brief and every model-written text is a judgement and is marked so. No automated step signs.
  • An answer and a signature name a GitHub login through the approval key, as a v3.33 approval does; the network holds no email and the notice uses GitHub's own delivery.
  • Every Lean the job writes is elaborated before it is committed (2026-09-17); a candidate's Lean is elaborated only in the sandbox (D-4, C8).
  • The blind rule is a service rule over a public graph: the record says what a person declared, never what the network verified.
  • The App's issue comment is a write to the graph repository's issues under the App; its content is the questions' words and a link, never a token or a login beyond the mention.

9. Open questions / judgment calls

IDEntry
F26-Q1Numbering. The draft called itself v3.36; by the time the owner approved building it (2026-10-12), v3.37 and v3.38 were applied, so it is applied as v3.39, the draft's file keeps its name, and the doc's version history says so (the 2026-10-12 log entry's rule: claim the number on main before building).
F26-Q2Rebuild, do not import, the prototypes. They drove the hosted fast check from a laptop and simulated the proposer in Python; the job runs in the sandbox with its own budget and reads real answer records. Their test corpora (candidates, planted defects, mutants and the expected counts) become the fixtures, so the rebuild is held to the measured results (AC6).
F26-Q3Records before routes. Every review act is a record on the graph first; the service only opens pull requests (J6 of F25's reasoning). The job's output is a bot commit like the post-merge job's holes, with the same idempotence rule (F07-T21's lesson about a guard doing a second job).
F26-Q4Definitions keep F12's pass for now. D-9 names definitions as a review object; the review here is of roots. A definition review would need its own probes (equality of the defined object), which the prototypes did not test; it waits on evidence.
F26-Q5Who is the intent signer for an inherited conjecture with no steward? The amendment says the steward; most live targets have none. The review still runs and its questions wait, shown on the target page as "needs an intent signer", which is one more reason a problem needs a steward (D-32), and the state says so rather than the review pretending to be complete.
F26-Q6Open pair directions are not frontier entries. The frontier is nodes (D-25); a pair direction is work on a review. They are listed on the review page, in list_open_pairs and in context, and a proof of one is a pair-proof artifact credited on a new ledger line kind, pair; adding them to the frontier would be a D-25 change and is not proposed.
F26-Q7The background review must not move anything on its own. On an existing target it opens, runs, and files what it finds through D-15 and D-8; it never changes a grade, a status or a frontier entry (AC18 measures that no grade moves). Its one visible effect is the state line and, if a question arises, a notice to the steward where one exists.
F26-Q8The ladder is climbed in order (T2). R9's table read as rungs in sequence (F24-Q7's rule): screened-and-signed needs the review complete and one accept from someone who wrote no surviving candidate; author-attested adds the intent signer's accept; expert-attested both roles and a person-written candidate in the cluster, the cap holding every rung above two. So an intent signer who alone wrote the surviving candidate and is the only signer earns nothing above the floor until a non-author signs, and then both rungs at once. One condition in review.earned to reverse. grade_of never returns below the held grade; the live measurement that no grade moves is T14's.
F26-Q9The latest signature per role decides (T2). A send-back after an accept withdraws the acceptance from the products and the ladder, since a withdrawn acceptance still shown as a signer would be published state unlike actual state; the held grade is never lowered by it. A split is read only from the intent signer's latest signature. Pair records are read per (a, b) with the latest date winning; exhibits are JSON (T1's YAML-only reader could never have seen them); a candidate that a format exhibit returns outranks one an exhibit discards, and an error exhibit decides nothing. AC2's "discarded with its exhibit" lives on review.derive(), which the page reads; the product object carries counts only. Every rule has a mutant that kills it (53, all killed).
F26-Q10The brief has no record, and the page says so (T12). R16's "screens and the brief" straddles the exhibit/judgement line: screens are exhibits, and the review's schemas carry no brief (F12's lives under qa/briefs/). The page's fifth section is an exhibit section with the brief as a marked judgement aside saying the review's records carry none; whether a brief ever belongs to the review (the signer reads the Lean, R10) or a ninth record is wanted is the owner's. The page derives survivors and clusters from the records itself ("from the record only"); the state line uses the product's words, and since T2 the two agree. Mutant exhibits: fail means the tests caught it, pass that it survived and became a question (the schema description now says so, T5).
F26-Q11Infix concepts cannot be wrapped automatically (T4). The wrapper delegates a concept only when the original writes it as an identifier or a single-constant notation (Nat.Prime, φ); p ∧ q or a ∣ b has no textual rewrite that keeps precedence without a parser, and a wrapper the toolchain accepts on a wrong restatement is worse than none, so such a concept is refused by name and the opener names it another way or writes the wrapper by hand. The rule reads two candidate shapes (a sorry-bodied theorem, or a Prop-valued def as the prototypes wrote); a declared predicate the claim never uses is returned too, since the probes would have nothing to replace; the wrapper emits Is<Concept>, never the bare name, because under open Nat a local Prime is ambiguous. The second prototype's "format" named parts (member, domain), not concepts: which the manifest holds for those targets is settled when their corpus becomes T5's fixtures (Q2).
F26-Q12Who opens, as built (T3). The policy is read from the merge's parent tree, so a pull request cannot widen the setting for itself; network cannot be listed in review_openers (the schema enumerates three roles) and a network opening by pull request is refused by name. Opened-twice compares supersedes to the existing review's source.ref (the root version), checkable only when a base is given; the words fall back informal → the root's doc comment → the paraphrase → refuse (a live root with none of these is refused by --all, to check before T14); the ported candidate's author is the root's statement-line identity else the literal curator, and R2's "credited to the source's authors" for a registry statement is one function to widen. --date takes a day because the record carries one.
F26-Q13The routes as built (T10). Opening, a candidate, an answer and a signature each open one pull request carrying exactly the record and refuse by name before one opens; the blind rule withholds every other candidate's Lean (lean: null, lean_withheld: "blind") from a caller whose pseudonym has no candidate on the record; a candidate must declare read_others (candidate-not-blind otherwise). review-opener-not-allowed is emitted by both the gate (policy, from the parent tree) and the service (the login's roles), so its catalog row is both: the merge of T3 and T10 had defined it twice, which a Python dict accepts silently, and the resolution is one row. The tools-list listing grew from 94,398 to 105,034 characters with the four tools and is pinned at 106,000. The T10 agent stopped on a spend limit after its evidence had run; the lead committed its worktree as it stood, every check re-run first.
