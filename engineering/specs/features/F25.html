F25 — Registration in Palomar

A resolved problem can be registered in Palomar, the registry of Lean-verified mathematics, from a wrapper the pinned gate exports. The record learns what the registry needs: a structured disclosure of models and tooling, a classification, a display name a contributor may choose, and a registration record. Registered entries are re-checked with the network's own tools, and the registry's feed is read for prior art.

1. Summary

Palomar (palomar-registry.org, opened 2026-08-18 by the Lean FRO and ICARM) indexes a public GitHub repository at one commit: a Challenge.lean stating the result with sorry, a Solution.lean proving it, a comparator.json, a formalization.yaml (v0.4) and one root licence file; every Lean file on Lean's module system; toolchain at least leanprover/lean4:v4.35.0-rc2; Comparator with two independent kernels; a model's editorial review with a notability floor; humans only as authors, with AI disclosed under automation.methods. Decisions v3.37 (D-10, D-23, D-19, D-32, D-3, D-25, D-28, D-35) is the protocol; this feature builds it:

  • The record says what the registry asks. submission-meta/v2 adds an automation block (method, models, framework required; cost optional) carried into attestation/v7 and ledger/v2; target/v3 adds a classification; registration/v1 and display-name/v1 are two append-only records.
  • The export. opn-gate export-palomar writes a wrapper repository from one resolved target at the graph's pin and checks it there; a port stage moves it to the registry's toolchain and the registry's own preflight workflow is the definition of done.
  • The corpus. Seven registered entries are re-checked with the network's tools, so a disagreement between the network's checkers and the registry's is found before any registration depends on them.
  • The feed. The registry's CC0 feed is matched against the network's problems and proposes literature records; a steward confirms or not.
  • The surfaces. Two routes and three tools, the site's target and Docs pages, the guide, the error catalog and the DCO text.

2. User stories

  • As a curator, I export the resolved target, port it to the registry's toolchain, run the registry's preflight, read its review, and register it; the target page then names the registration, and the ledger credits it on the upstreaming line.
  • As a contributor whose proof is in the closure, I am an author of the registration under my pseudonym; if I record a display name, that is what the registry shows.
  • As an agent submitting a proof, I declare how I worked (agent, the models, the harness) in one block the precheck validates before anything opens; I may add my token counts.
  • As a steward, I am shown that the registry's feed names my problem, with the evidence, and I confirm or dismiss the literature proposal.
  • As the owner, I run the corpus job and see the network's own checkers agree with the registry on seven entries at the registry's toolchain.

3. Scope

In: decisions v3.37; the schemas above and targets-index/v10, graph/v7; the gate's admission of the two records; the export, check, port, verify and feed commands with a client seam; the service's two routes and the automation refusal; three MCP tools; the site's registration line, Docs and About copy; the guide; the conformance corpus and its workflow; the rerun of the three resolved targets.

Out: a newer toolchain pin for the graphs or for new intakes (the owner's later call); nanoda or con-ron inside the gate; a contributor tool that submits to the registry; any notability score in the network (D-25); importing registry entries as graph nodes; registering euclid-primes or tutorial; a human reading of the erdos-1050 port's review, which is the owner's (Q3).

The gate shall admit one only from a listed curator or a steward of the target. The latest record per registry id shall be the one read; a withdrawal record (D-18 v3.27) shall set one aside.
F25-R6A display-name record (names/<pseudonym>.yaml, display-name/v1) shall carry the pseudonym, a name of at most 200 characters or null, an optional link, a date and an approval-key signature. F25-R7targets/index.json at targets-index/v10 and targets/<id>/graph.json at graph/v7 shall publish each target's registrations: registry, id, version, status and url, derived from R5.
F25-R8opn-gate export-palomar <target> shall write, from the graph at one commit, a wrapper repository in the registry's shape: licence, lakefile with the graph's Mathlib pin, synthesised manifest, toolchain, Challenge.lean (the root's statement with the target's definitions inlined), Solution.lean (the root's proof), one module per node of the proof's closure, comparator.json, formalization.yaml validated against the registry's vendored schema, a README giving the narrative production account, a preflight workflow, and opn-export.json naming the graph commit, the closure, the pruned dependencies and the hash of every file in and out. Every Lean file shall begin with module and use public import and an exposed public section; bodies shall be byte-identical to the graph's.
F25-R9The export shall refuse, by name: a root that is not proved; a proof record that is not a proof; a closure node whose trust base is the compiler; a target with no classification; a graph commit with no root licence file; an unproved dependency; a foreign import; a private helper used across modules; a Challenge over 1,000 lines or 100 KiB, or any file over 10,000 lines; a sorry outside the Challenge; and, when Comparator does not compare definition bodies (Q1), a target with definitions.
F25-R10The export's closure shall start from the proof's footprint and keep a declared dependency outside it only when the importing proof's text names its declaration; the README shall list what was pruned and why.
F25-R11With --check, the export shall elaborate every module at the graph's pin, replay each through the kernel, check the Solution's axioms against the allowlist, check by the kernel that the Solution's theorem has the Challenge's type, and check that the Challenge's theorem text equals the node's statement text; it shall write local-check.json and exit non-zero on any failure.
F25-R12With --port, the tool shall rewrite the toolchain and the Mathlib revision, update the manifest, build, run lake comparator, classify failures into port-report.json, and refuse when the Challenge's bytes have changed. It shall parse the registry's preflight artifact into findings and remedies. The registry's preflight at status: pass is the definition of a port's completion.
F25-R13opn-gate palomar-verify <repo>@<commit> shall clone a registered entry, check its challenge and solution hashes against the registry's record, build it, run lake comparator, and run the network's axioms and statement-meaning checkers built at the entry's toolchain; it shall write conformance-<id>.json and exit non-zero on any disagreement. A workflow shall run it over the corpus on dispatch and weekly, and report drift in the registry's pins.
F25-R14opn-gate palomar-feed shall read the registry's feed through the client seam, match entries to the network's targets by problem number as a token, report candidates with evidence, write unsigned literature proposals only with --propose, never confirm, and, for each registration record, compare the registry's hashes and status with the record's. An unreachable feed shall write nothing and exit 1.
F25-R15The service shall accept POST /me/name from a bearer, writing R6's record for the bearer's own pseudonym and no other, and POST /registrations from a signed-in curator or steward, writing R5's record; each shall refuse by name what the gate would refuse, with nothing opened. The MCP shall expose set_display_name, register_target and list_registrations; get_target shall pass registrations through.
F25-R16GET /dco.json shall name Apache-2.0 for code and CDLA-Permissive-2.0 for the record; its version shall change, and a token issued against the older text shall be asked to accept again.
F25-R17The site shall show a target's registration (registered, or submitted and under review) on its page with a link to the registry entry, a Registries section and the two licences on the Docs page, and the registry by name in the About page's report-back sentence. Registry links shall pass the site's allowlist from validated records only.
F25-R18The guide shall document the automation block with an executing example, the DCO change, the registration section, the Palomar reference form in literature records, every new code, and a row per new tool; every route, tool, code and field the guide names shall exist, and every field R1 and R5 add shall be named in the guide and in the tool's input schema.

6. Budgets / limits

An export runs in seconds without --check; the check costs one elaboration per module at the pin. The corpus job pays one Mathlib cache fetch per entry and runs weekly. The feed is one GET of about 500 KiB. The registry allows two verifying submissions per repository owner and one per submitter; registrations are sequential. New routes share POST /stewards' rate limits; records are under 4 KiB.

7. Security & privacy notes

  • An agent never registers. The client seam's register refuses without a flag only the CLI sets when a person says they have read the review; the registry's own protocol says the same.
  • A display name is a one-way publication. It is written only by the holder of the pseudonym, withdrawal exists from the first version, the guide warns before the call, and the site shows it nowhere in this version.
  • Vendored material is licensed. Corpus fixtures carry a NOTICE; nothing off intake.LICENCE_ALLOWLIST is vendored. The registry's schema and policy are CC0.
  • Free text is escaped. Names, notes and urls pass the same allowlists as every other record; registry urls become links only from validated records.
  • No secret. Preflight needs contents: read; the submission token the registry issues is the curator's, held outside the network.

9. Open questions / judgment calls

Each weighed against the project's goals: the mission (D-1); published state is actual state, trackable, reversible, hard to tamper with; the graph as the only truth (D-35); an inclusive pseudonymous ledger with no agent identities and no ranking (D-19, D-22, D-25); Leiden alignment and honest report-back (D-10, D-23); agent-friendliness; a statement published is a statement checked (D-9).

F25-Q1Definitions duplicated in Challenge and development. For: the current candidate needs none and registered entries inline definitions. Against: if Comparator compares only types, a Solution could redefine a definition and prove a weaker thing, the failure D-9 exists to prevent. Decided: probe first (T2); if bodies are compared, duplicate; if not, refuse exports with definitions.
F25-Q2module at the 4.33.1 pin. For checking the exact bytes at the pin: one artifact, one claim. Against: the pin may reject it. Decided: probe; if rejected, check a plain-header twin and record "bodies identical, headers differ"; Comparator at the registry's toolchain still checks the final bytes.
F25-Q3Submit erdos-1050 now? For: a live end-to-end run. Against: it is a calibration target, a known result reproved by agents; the registry refuses duplicated work, and the 2026-09-11 declaration the project answers to condemns mass production of statements; a refusal would be the network's first public act there. Decided: export, port and preflight, do not submit; the first submission is the first open-track resolution. The owner may override.
F25-Q4Pseudonymous authors. For: D-19 and D-22 make the pseudonym the identity and the ledger the credit; a human behind a pseudonym is a human. Against: the registry may read "humans only" as "named humans". Decided: pseudonyms with opt-in names (ruled 2026-10-10); the authors list is one function, so a refusal costs one change.
F25-Q5Where the wrapper repository lives. The network's account: the curator is the maintainer the registry requires, one place to preserve, one preflight. Contributors are the authors in the metadata and the ledger credits the registration.
F25-Q6Records on the graph, not in the service store. D-35: the service may be lost without any fact changing; git history is the trail; a later record is the reversal; signatures are the tamper evidence. A display name has a withdrawal from day one.
F25-Q7Mandatory disclosure against agent-friendliness. Leiden and the registry both ask for it, and the free string has produced undeclared on 46 statement lines. Decided (ruled 2026-10-10): mandatory, refused at precheck before any pull request, with a one-line example in the guide and the tool schema.
F25-Q8Prune declared dependencies to the footprint. The proof term is the fact (F18) and the trusted surface is smaller; the wrapper's module list differs from the graph's declared deps, so the README states the rule and lists what was pruned.
F25-Q9Old merges have no structured disclosure or cost. A value nobody recorded is a fabrication on a permanent record. Decided: map the string to a method only where the string says it, and write "not reconstructed" in the registry's own words.
F25-Q10The network's metaprograms at the registry's toolchain. Built in the corpus job only, never in the gate image; the pin stays 4.33.1.
F25-Q11Feed proposals never confirm. D-25 v3.35 makes confirmation a steward's or curator's act, and a number match is inference.
F25-Q12Policy drift and rate limits. The registry's pins, schemas and minimum toolchain move; PINS.json is re-probed weekly by the corpus job; registrations are sequential.
F25-Q13Numbering. v3.36 is reserved for the formalization review, drafted 2026-10-09 and not applied; this amendment is v3.37 so the two stay distinct. PROTOCOL_VERSION had lagged at 3.28 and is 3.37 now.
