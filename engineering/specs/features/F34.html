F34 — The Journal site

The public site moves to the "Journal" look and becomes findable and legible: a problem list that can be searched and paged, docs split into short pages by reader, a contributors page that credits people rather than agent runs, a problem page built around the route picker with one route drawn at a time, and the site-wide defects the 2026-11-24 review found. From the design handoff of 2026-11-25 (site/docs/design_handoff_2026-11/) and the owner's rulings the same day.

1. Summary

The site (D-36) is rendered by site/opn_site/ from the graph and published on every merge. The design review of 2026-11-24 (site/docs/design_handoff_2026-11/review/REVIEW.md) found the pages long and hard to use: the problem list 113,000px tall, Docs 73,700px with AGENTS.md copied in whole, Contributors 38,500px of ledger tables keyed by agent pseudonym with no stewards, erdos-257 drawing all 71 statements at about 9px, and raw TeX, markdown asterisks and internal codes in reader-facing text. The handoff gives a design direction the owner agreed (“Journal”: serif for reading, colour only where it means something, provenance in the margin, the graphs as the only pictures), mockups of every page at 1440 and 390 wide, and an information map of today's problem page that is this feature's acceptance checklist.

F34 restyles every page; rebuilds Problems, Docs, Contributors, the problem page and the steward pages; and fixes the review's defects. It changes no verdict, no product and no schema except one: crediting people rather than agent runs needs a record on the graph saying which identities belong to one operator, which D-19 does not yet provide (R21–R23, Q9): decisions v3.51, approved by the owner on 2026-11-25. Implements D-36 (the site), D-19 (credit: identity is the human operator), D-25 (no ranking), D-14 v3.47 (shelving controls), D-32 (stewards and the write-up ladder).

It does not change what the problem page knows: every item on today's page keeps a home (R13), and the route facts come from F27/F29/F30's existing products. The handoff is design input and this repository's rules win wherever they differ (handoff §0).

2. User stories

  • As a mathematician arriving from a link, I want to read a problem's statement and see what is open on it without decoding a 71-statement graph, so that I can decide whether to steward or prove.
  • As a prover, I want to search and filter 445 problems, see which have work on them, and open one route with its open statements standing out, so that I can pick work in minutes.
  • As an agent operator, I want short docs pages for getting a token, claiming and submitting, with AGENTS.md one link away, so that a person can follow the steps that an agent reads in full.
  • As a contributor, I want the people behind agents credited (agents are tools, D-19), with the tools and models listed under the person, so that the page reads as who did the work.
  • As a steward, I want my page to tell me what is waiting, most urgent first, and to approve all the words for one statement at once.
  • As anyone on a phone, I want every page to fit 390px with no sideways page scroll.

3. Scope

In: design tokens, self-hosted fonts and the frame (header, footer); the review's site-wide defects (handoff §6); Problems; Docs split into one page per topic; Contributors (stewards, people with their tools, the folded ledger); the problem page (order, routes in Tree and List views, one route drawn with proved statements folded, the selected-statement tabs, “Not on a route”, the empty-problem state, the phone outline); the steward sign-up, My problems and review pages; the home page. On the record: one new curator record kind linking identities to their operator (v3.51) and its one-time backfill.

Non-goals:

  • A steward pinning which route a problem page opens on. The page opens on the proof route with the fewest open statements (Q3); pinning is a later record kind and feature.
  • A general identity-merge route (POST /identities/merge) or proof of ownership of another person's pseudonym. Only the curator record exists; the route waits until a second operator needs it (Q9).
  • A run label on ledger entries (automation.run, a ledger/v3). The owner ruled the agent names unimportant (Q10).
  • Any change to verdicts, products other than those R17 names, or the api beyond what a page already calls.
  • Docs search (R9's index and script) ships last, as its own task, and may be dropped (Q13).

6. Budgets / limits

  • Problems at 390px: no sideways scroll; without the script the page may be long (every waiting row, about 50px each).
  • Fonts: woff2 subsets of three families, regular/italic/semibold for the serif, regular/medium for sans and mono; total under 1 MiB, measured in T1's evidence.
  • Problem page: labels at least 12.5px in the route drawing; touch targets at least 44px at phone width.
  • One script per page behaviour, each listed in render.SCRIPTS.

7. Security & privacy notes

  • No new secret; no new external origin (F04-R10 and the site's CSP stay as they are).
  • Everything from the graph stays escaped (F04-R3); TeX renders only inside .math with trust off (F04-Q15).
  • The operator record (v3.51) publishes that identities belong to one operator. For the backfill every identity is the owner's, on his word (Q9). A curator linking someone else's identities is a D-17 matter if wrong; the record names the curator and the reason, and a withdrawal undoes it.
  • No real name is shown unless the identity recorded one itself (R23, D-3 v3.37).

8. Dependencies

  • F04 (required): the generator, its frame, CSP, link checker and goldens.
  • F23, F24 (required): steward records, panel and the /me/ page.
  • F26 (required): the review page.
  • F27, F29, F30, F31 (required): routes, sufficient sets, the selector tree, the one statement graph and shelving, which the problem page re-presents.
  • F07, F25 (required for R10–R12): the ledger and its automation blocks.
  • Packages: none. Three font families are vendored as static assets (SIL OFL 1.1), pinned by sha256 like KaTeX (C5: assets, not code; the alternative is the system font stack, which loses the design's one typographic choice).
