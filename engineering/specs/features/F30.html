F30 — One statement graph

The problem page's two drawings (the statement graph of F04 and the routes drawing of F27/F29) become one: every statement and every route junction in one picture, proved read from the pill itself, the sufficient sets chosen on a small OR tree drawn as nodes and edges, and a selection that fades what it drops and slides what it keeps into a tree, so a reader zooms into one piece of the proof skeleton (the owner's request of 2026-10-18).

1. Summary

On 2026-10-18 the owner asked for the two graphs on a problem page to be one view. From the statement graph (F04-T12, T33): clicking a statement shows its record in the side panel, and hovering lights its lines and the statements they join. From the routes drawing (F27-T3, F29-T6): the sufficient sets and the choosing of them. Three things change shape. The selector stops being an indented list and becomes the OR tree itself, nodes and edges, where a leaf is one set, an interior node is a family of sets (every set beneath it) and an edge is the commitment "close this statement by this route", which fixes the skeleton down to the next decision. Proved reads from the pill: a proved statement is a solid filled pill, an open one is outlined, and the set colours, pies and wedges go. And a selection moves the drawing: what is not in the selection fades, then what remains closes its gaps into a tree; moving from one set to a neighbour slides little, because every layout orders rows from the full graph's own order and every node keeps one identity across layouts.

The owner's rulings, taken in the planning conversation: the selector's nodes and edges never touch the side panel, and only a click on the main drawing does; the one view holds everything the statement graph held, with the statements on no route hidden behind a toggle; faded nodes leave and the tree compacts; proved is a filled pill; hover anywhere on the selector previews on the main drawing with no movement, and only a click transitions; clicking a selector edge shows the committed part (the holes the route fixes that lead nowhere else are included, Q3), cut above at the statement the edge is a route of and below at the next fork; the AND dots and OR diamonds stay. Left to the builder and taken as follows: the F18 proof buttons fold into the selector as proof leaves (Q4); the sets table keeps its sorting and "Fewest open first" and sits below the drawing (Q5); a set's tree shows its assemblies and junctions (Q6).

2. User stories

  • As a reader of a problem page, I want one drawing of the problem's statements in which proved and unproved are told apart at a glance, so that I do not read two pictures of the same graph.
  • As a reader, I want to click a statement and read its record beside the drawing, and to hover one and see what rests on it and what it rests on, as the page does today.
  • As an operator choosing where to send agents, I want to pick a sufficient set, or a family of sets, or one commitment, on a small tree of the choices, and watch the drawing become the tree of just that work, so that I can zoom into one piece of the skeleton and compare neighbours without the picture jumping.
  • As a reader on a phone or without JavaScript, I want the drawing, the selector and the table to be complete and still, with every statement a link to its record.

3. Scope

In: site/opn_site/one_view.py (the merged drawing, the selector drawing, the layouts, the table), one change to dag.layout (an order hint), the page's script static/graph.js replacing problem.js and routes.js, the stylesheet, the template, the key, the restated tests, screenshots on a full render of the live graph. Spec amendments: F27-R10 and F29-R10 are superseded by F30-R5 to R9 (Q1); F18-R3's buttons by F30-R6 (Q4).

Non-goals:

  • No change to which sets exist, their order, or the selector tree's construction (opn_gate.routes, F27-R6, R7, F29-R6): the page draws what the tool returns.
  • No change to any product, schema or MCP tool; nothing to re-pin.
  • No ranking of sets or statements (D-25).
  • No new layout algorithm: every layout is dag.layout over a subset, seeded with the full layout's order (Q2).

The side panel is not changed by any selector click.
F30-R8When a selector node or edge, or a table row, is hovered or focused, the main drawing shall light every node and line of that view and dim the rest, with no coordinate changed and no fade; leaving restores the selection's state. A node absent from the current layout is not drawn and so not lit.
F30-R9The sets table (F27-R11, with its sorting and "Fewest open first") shall sit below the drawing beside the selector, filtered to the selected view's sets and saying "showing n of m"; a row click selects that set's leaf view.
F30-R10Across every pair of views the statements shared by both shall keep their left-to-right order within each layer, except where keeping it would make two lines cross in that view; and a view's layout shall have no overlapping pills and every node one layer below the lowest node that rests on it (F04-T33's properties).
F30-R12When a pill is hovered, the lighting shall reach through every route junction it touches: a statement's hover lights its junctions, the lines beyond them and the holes they lead to, and a hole's hover its junction and parent (the owner, 2026-10-18: the hover "stopped at the intermediate nodes").
F30-R13The selector shall be a small, typical tree diagram at the top of the drawing's card, in view while the drawing scrolls: a dot per node, straight lines, leaves numbered by set, the root statement above the top split when they differ, no words drawn; a node's words (what it is and the route its edge chose) show on hover and in its title.
F30-R14When a selector node is hovered or focused, the selector shall light it, its lines and the nodes they join and dim the rest of the selector, beside the preview it casts on the drawing (R8); leaving clears both. An edge keeps its own hover stroke and the preview and lights no neighbour (T9).
F30-R15The selector's panel shall say it is the navigation device (select a node or an edge to filter the drawing) and its heading shall carry the glossary's hover card for a sufficient set; no mark in the selector shall be a filled dot, which the drawing uses for proved.
F30-R11Without the script the page shall show the routes layout, the selector and the full table, every pill and selector node a link; with it, nothing is rendered twice.

6. Budgets / limits

Views number the selector's nodes plus edges plus proofs plus two; on erdos-1094 about thirty. The layouts JSON carries each node once per view and each line's bend points (not its sampled curve), so it stays under a few hundred kilobytes on the largest live target; the test records the size. The transition is 200 ms fade, 400 ms move, 200 ms fade, zero under reduced motion.

7. Security & privacy notes

Labels and approaches are contributor text, escaped in markup and in the JSON (which carries ids and numbers only, never words). No external resource (F04-R10). No new route, record or permission.

8. Dependencies

F04 (the page and the layout), F27 and F29 (routes, sets, the selector tree), F18 (proofs and their marks), F28 (closable). Live after a network deploy of the site; no graph change.

9. Open questions / judgment calls

F30-Q1 — F27-R10 and F29-R10 give way. Both say the drawing's layout never depends on what is selected, and R10 made each statement a pie of set colours. The owner asked for the opposite: a selection becomes a tree. Those two lines are superseded by R5 to R9 here; the rest of F27 and F29 stand. What that rule protected, that a reader can find a statement where it was, is kept by R10's order property and by the return to the routes layout on clearing.

F30-Q2 — No new layout; a seed. A tidy tree per view is dag.layout over the view's nodes, which is already a layered layout with crossing minimisation. What keeps shared nodes still is the initial order of every rank: seeded from the full layout's x-order instead of lexical ids, so the trunk and shared subtrees come out in the same order in every view. R10 tests the property, not a pixel budget (2026-10-04: a distance proxy measured the wrong thing). On the live erdos-1094 the seed took the disagreeing pairs from 191 to 1, and the one left is a view that swaps two pills to avoid a crossing of its own; crossing-free outranks still, so R10 allows exactly that, and the test shows the crossing by pinning the shared order.

F30-Q3 — A segment is the committed part, not the bare chain. A route may have holes that lead to no further choice; choosing the route commits to them, so the segment includes them. The owner's ruling.

F30-Q4 — Proofs are leaves. A proof of a resolved root is a fully proved sufficient set, so it is one more leaf of the selector rather than a row of buttons above the drawing. Its view is F18's closure with the edges the term follows; the proof note and the reading link show beneath the selector when it is selected.

F30-Q5 — The table stays, below. Sorting, "Fewest open first" and the "which" lists are kept; it moves from the right column to under the drawing, beside the selector, since the right column is the panel's.

F30-Q6 — A set's tree keeps its junctions. The owner asked to keep the AND dots and OR diamonds; a set's view therefore holds its assemblies and route junctions, so the tree reads as the proof skeleton rather than a bag of leaves.

F30-Q7 — The approach rides the child, not the edge. R5 said each selector edge is labelled by its route's approach. On the live erdos-1094 the two edges out of a fork share a midpoint and their labels were written over each other; since every child has exactly one incoming edge, the approach is the child pill's second line (text.sel-approach), the full text in its title, and the pill's width is measured from it so the layout spaces siblings apart. Measured on the live render: no selector text leaves its pill.

F30-Q8 — The loose group follows the layout that has it. The divider and label of the statements no line joins were rendered only when the opening layout had such a group; the toggle's own case (every variant off-route) had none to move. They are rendered from the first layout that has a group, hidden until the script moves to it (the script builder's finding).

F30-Q9 — The hash on load. problem.js never wrote the hash on load and wrote node= for the selected panel on every change; graph.js keeps both, adding view= only when the view is not routes, so a bookmarked page opens as it did.

F30-Q10 — Three browser tests fail before and after. With Playwright installed, test_web_flows.py's two gloss-control tests and test_finding_lemma_edge.py's record-page measurement time out on origin/main exactly as on this branch (run in a detached worktree with the same Playwright 1.64.0); they are not F30's and are left as found.

F30-Q11 — The selector scrolls on a phone. At 390px the selector shrank to its card and its words became unreadable; it now scrolls sideways inside its card, as the drawing does (the routes drawing had the same rule).

F30-Q12 — The owner's second reading (2026-10-18, after the first deploy). The selector should be the top graphic, much smaller, in view while reading the dependency graph, more like a typical tree, with the words on hover; and the pill hover stopped at the junction dots. R12 to R14 and T6 to T8 are that reading. The selector's layout is no longer dag.layout but a plain tidy tree (leaves spaced in depth-first order, parents centred, a row per depth), pinned with position: sticky inside the drawing's card; the sets table stays beneath the drawing.

F30-Q13 — The owner's third reading (2026-10-18). Say that the tree is the navigation device and explain a sufficient set on hover; light neighbours for nodes only, not edges; and no filled dot in the selector, since the drawing's filled pill means proved. R15 and T9.

F30-Q14 — The owner's fourth reading (2026-10-18). An open circle at a fork of the selector read as the drawing's open statement: a fork is a small square now, the root a dashed square, a set its numbered circle; and a selected node or edge wears amber (--select), a colour nothing else on the page uses, so the selection is told from the hover's accent. T10.

F30-Q15 — The pointer is not a line (the owner, 2026-10-21). On erdos-257 a proved spec (uniqueness of representation) was joined to the unproved root by the dashed proposed-for edge, which carries no logical content, and the same edge pulled two specs into the row under the root as if they were its route while the gap lemma, which two proofs rest on, sank to the bottom. The owner: “I want users to see how specs relate to the nodes of the proof … give them a different location, not part of the proof tree, and hover/click on the spec to see how it relates.” So the band, below the tree (the lead’s call: the page is already a column, and a side band would squeeze the tree at phone width), and two kinds of relation shown with different weight — checked relations as lit lines, stated ones as words. The owner kept every spec on the band (“keep it simple”) rather than promoting a used one into the tree; the use edge is then one of its lit lines.
