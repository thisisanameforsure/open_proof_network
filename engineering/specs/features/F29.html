F29 — Labels, claims and the selector tree

Every statement gets a short name and its claim in notation, every route its approach, all written by the contributor who made it and filed as the gloss the record already has; the sufficient sets are navigated by a tree of the route choices, built from the sets and never from the graph (decisions v3.44, the owner's request of 2026-10-17).

1. Summary

On 2026-10-17 the owner read erdos-1094's frontier as an illegible mess: 43 open nodes against 11 proved, named erdos-1094--h2--h1--h4--h1--h2, with the sufficient-sets view lighting routes correctly and nothing saying what any route or node was doing. He asked for three things: a label and a one-line description per node, generated at creation and distinct from the explainer; intent notes at the branch points, readable by walking down the tree; and a condensed OR-only tree as the selector over the sets, each leaf one set, each interior node a family.

What the record already held settled most of the design. A one-line description of an open node is the statement gloss (gloss/v2, F20; D-3 v3.30): prose about one Lean file, on a node of any status, attributed, unverified, chained by supersedes, signed by a steward or curator, with F20-T16's convention that it opens with the statement in notation. Every hole already carries an author-chosen name in the partial that made it (h_bhp, h_strip: 90 holes across the graph, 5 generic) and the post-merge job already writes that name into the child's header. So the label rides the gloss as one new field, the claim is the gloss's opening display, and both are given at creation by the partial's own comments, which the service refuses before any precheck when they are missing. The OR fork's note cannot be written at creation, because a node's second route is written by someone who did not write the first; each route states its own approach and the fork shows them side by side (Q2). The selector tree is built from the enumerated sets rather than the unrolled graph, because a hole shared between branches (a lemma, D-12 v3.38) makes the unrolled OR tree over-count (Q1); under F27-T7's ruling the finest sets are one per choice of routes, so on a tree-shaped target the two agree, and on erdos-1094 both give 8.

The owner's rulings the same day: backfill by agents through the service with no human sign-off, stewards editing later; labels changed under the existing gloss rule; the table kept beneath the tree, the tree boxed as "Sufficient sets" inside the drawing where the set labels stood.

2. User stories

  • As a reader of a problem page, I want every statement drawn by a name and its claim in notation one click away, so that I can follow a decomposition without reading Lean or ids.
  • As an agent writing a skeleton, I want to say what each hole is and what attack I am taking where I write the Lean, and to be refused at once if I forget, so that my decomposition is legible to the next agent without a second submission.
  • As a steward, I want to rename a statement or correct its claim with the controls the gloss already has, so that no new record and no new permission is needed.
  • As an operator choosing where to send agents, I want to select "everything through the Granville–Ramaré approach" and see the sets beneath it lit on the full graph, coarse to fine.

3. Scope

In: decisions v3.44; gloss/v3 (label) and graph/v12 (label, label_source, claim; approach); gate/opn_gate/labels.py (the partial's header grammar, the fallbacks, the node's label); the gate's step-4 refusal and the hole writer filing each hole's gloss; the service's quick-fail; the selector tree in opn_gate.routes, the MCP tool fields and the problem page's drawing and panel; the guide; the backfill of every live node.

Non-goals:

  • No note on an edge, no record on a fork (Q2).
  • No label or claim derived from the Lean by the network: a Lean-to-notation translation is the unverified step the fidelity evidence is about, so the words are a contributor's, attributed, and the gloss carries the hash of the Lean it describes (D-3 v3.44).
  • No new write tool, route or record kind: a label is changed by submit_gloss.
  • No change to CONTEXT.json, which has two builders that must agree (F10-Q7): the tool reads label and claim from graph.json.
  • No change to which sets are published or their order (F27-R6, R7).

6. Budgets / limits

A label is at most 60 characters, an approach 300, a claim 4000. The selector is linear in the number of sets times the depth of the longest route list; the finest sets number in the tens. The text scan of a partial is one pass over its lines and runs before any host call.

7. Security & privacy notes

Every label, claim and approach is contributor text: demarcated untrusted data on the MCP (D-28), escaped on the site, rendered as math only inside the one element class the renderer may touch (F04-R10's KaTeX rule, trust off). The hole writer files words the author wrote under the licence the author named; it writes none of its own. No new secret, route or permission.

8. Dependencies

F20 (glosses), F27 (routes and sets), F18 (decompositions in the products), F07 (the hole writer), F13 (the service's pre-flight refusals), F04 (the site). Live after a network deploy and a graph re-pin; the backfill follows the re-pin, since only then may a gloss carry a label.

9. Open questions / judgment calls

F29-Q1 — The selector is built from the sets, not the graph. The request described an OR-only collapse of the AND-OR graph. On erdos-1094 route h3@2 restates h2--h1@1's hole as a lemma, so under the unrolled tree the shared subtree appears twice and the leaves over-count what the sets are; had the shared node its own routes, the copies could be chosen differently, a plan nobody executes. Splitting the published sets on their first differing choice gives a tree whose leaves are the sets by construction, whatever the sharing. Under F27-T7's ruling (every choice of routes is a set) the two constructions coincide on today's live targets, which is why the request's picture was right and the construction still had to change.

F29-Q2 — No edge notes; the fork is derived. An OR fork's "A vs B" cannot be written at creation, because B does not exist when A is written and a note on B goes stale at C. Each route declares its own approach; the fork shows them side by side; an AND fork is its route's hole labels. The owner raised the doubt about edge-level notes and agreed with this reading.

F29-Q3 — The claim is written, never translated. A Lean-to-notation translation by the network would be exactly the unverified step the 2026 fidelity evidence is about (D-9 v3.12). The author writes the claim beside the Lean; the gloss carries the Lean's hash; a steward signs.

F29-Q4 — The licence rides the partial. The service refuses an absent licence rather than defaulting one (F05-T17), and a plain-git partial has no request parameters, so the one home for the words' licence is a -- licence: line in the partial, beside the annex citation. opn_gate.labels.LICENCES is the one list; the service reads it.

F29-Q5 — Backfill through the service, no human sign-off. The owner's ruling: the backfill fills the labels and claims; a human approving them is not necessary; stewards edit later. Glosses are appends with no step 9, so the merge actor merges them unattended; each is attributed to its agent's pseudonym with drafted_with, unverified until signed.

F29-Q6 — Who renames. The existing gloss rule (anyone proposes a superseding version; a steward's or curator's own is final; a person's change over verified words is pending until signed). The owner chose it over a steward-only rule.

F29-Q7 — The table stays, filtered. The owner: keep the table; put the tree in the drawing where the set list was, boxed and headed "Sufficient sets".

F29-Q8 — label_source has no proposal value. The amendment's first draft named a variant's "proposal name"; no proposal carries one, and an enum value nothing emits is a live bug (2026-09-10). A variant falls back to its id.
