F23 — Stewards and curators on the site

Sign in with GitHub. Become a problem's steward in one short form. Edit and approve the words on the page you are reading. No SSH key, no curl, no long document to read first.

1. Summary

On 2026-10-06 the owner found four problems on the site:

  • Nobody can sign in.
  • "Needs a steward" is only a filter.
  • "Become its steward" and "How stewardship works" land on the long Docs page.
  • The only way to edit or approve words is the service's routes, through curl or an MCP client. A steward record and every signature also need the steward's own SSH key.

F23 builds the flow the owner approved on 2026-10-07:

  • a Sign in link on every page;
  • a one-screen steward form that does on GitHub what a filled-in form would lead to (it opens the record's pull request);
  • buttons that depend on the reader's role on the pages that show words.

The protocol side is decisions v3.33 (D-3, D-22, D-28, D-32, D-35) and one C8 entry, drafted in engineering/session-notes/2026-10-07-amendment-v3.33-stewards-on-the-site.md. Nothing here is built until v3.33 is applied (T0).

2. User stories

  • As a mathematician who sees "Needs a steward", I click it, sign in with GitHub, tick one box and become the steward. I see my name on the problem a few minutes later, and I never read the Docs page unless I want to.
  • As a steward, the words on my problem's pages show me Approve and Edit. A page lists the drafts waiting for me, and I can step down from the same place.
  • As anyone signed in, I can draft words for any Lean file. The page shows my draft as a draft until a steward or curator approves it or rewrites it.
  • As the owner, I see who admitted themselves as a steward. I can switch admission to reviewed later without changing any record.
  • As a reader, I can tell final words from drafts, and a self-admitted steward from a reviewed one.

3. Scope

In:

  • The service: web sign-in and sessions, GET /session, POST /session/end, POST /stewards, POST /approvals, and the words routes accepting a web session.
  • The gate: the new record versions and the approval key, and the policy switch.
  • The site:
    • the header sign-in;
    • /steward/ and /steward/<target>/;
    • /me/;
    • edit and approve controls on node and proof pages;
    • the draft, final and self-admitted labels.
  • The graph: keys/approval.pub, policy.json's new field, and curators.json unchanged with the owner in it.
  • The merge actor: under reviewed, it leaves steward records to a curator.
  • The guide: the stewards section, shortened.

Out:

  • MCP tools for either role;
  • a curator self-service route;
  • any vetting of stewards beyond the switch;
  • sign-in without GitHub;
  • email of any kind;
  • proposing a new problem from the site (the graph's issue form stays).

F23-R2When GET /auth/github/start is called with return, the service shall accept only a path on the site's own origin. After GitHub, it shall send the browser to that path with a web session set, and show no token and no pseudonym screen. On a first sign-in, the service shall ask once for the DCO acceptance (one tick box) and shall make the pseudonym the GitHub login. A login that already has an identity shall get that identity.
F23-R3A web session shall be an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict cookie on the service's host, valid for eight hours (config). The service shall accept it only on a request whose Origin is the site's origin and which carries the X-OPN-Web: 1 header, and only on GET /session, POST /session/end, POST /stewards, POST /approvals and the words routes (POST /glosses, POST /glosses/withdrawals). Every other route shall ignore it.
F23-R4GET /session shall answer the login, whether they are a curator (curators.json), and the targets on which they are an active steward, all read from the graph at the service's current commit. With no session it shall answer {"signed_in": false}.
F23-R5On Home and Problems, "Needs a steward" shall be a link with its count to /steward/. That page shall list the targets that have no active steward, one line each, every line with a Steward this button to /steward/<target>/. The Problems filter shall stay.
F23-R6Every "Become its steward" and "Propose or steward" link to a known target shall go to /steward/<target>/, never to the Docs page.
F23-R7/steward/<target>/ shall fit on one screen at 1440 and show:
  • the problem's statement in prose;
  • what the role asks, in three lines;
  • a "Read more" link to the Docs anchor;
  • display name (prefilled from GitHub) and identity link (optional while admission is open);
  • the commitment sentence verbatim with a tick box;
  • one Become steward button.
Signed out, the button shall be Sign in with GitHub to continue and return to the same page.
F23-R8POST /stewards shall write steward/v2 as the session's login, sign it with the approval key and open its pull request with the identity as author (D-23).
  • Under open, it shall use an append/ branch and carry admitted_by: self.
  • Under reviewed, it shall use a curate/ branch, which the merge actor does not take.
It shall answer the pull request's URL. A login already active on the target shall get 409. A step-down from a login that is not active shall get 409.
F23-R9The gate shall admit a steward/v2 record only when:
  • its signature verifies under the keys/approval.pub in the merge's parent tree;
  • its admitted_by agrees with the policy in that tree: self only under open; under reviewed, a login in curators.json, and the pull request is merged by that curator.
steward/v1 records stay valid. A step-down of either version may end a commitment of either version for the same login.
F23-R10POST /approvals shall take a gloss or explainer version and the sections to approve, from a session whose login is a steward of the version's target or a curator. It shall write gloss-signature/v3 or explainer-signature/v3 (signer login, via: approval-key), sign it with the approval key, and open its pull request on an append/ branch. Any other login shall get 403.
F23-R11The gate and the products shall treat a v3 signature like an SSH-signed one in every rule that reads signatures (verified sections, write-up credit, digestion). Following D-3 v3.33, a version signed by its own author, who is a steward of the target or a curator, shall be verified and shall earn once, as written work.
F23-R12On node and proof pages, every gloss and explainer section shall show its state: Draft (with its author), Pending (with whose change), or Final (with the approver and date). With a session:
  • anyone shall see Edit, which opens a text box holding the current words and a live preview through the words route's dry_run;
  • a steward of the target or a curator shall also see Approve on each section that is not final;
  • a curator shall also see Withdraw.
Submitting shall show the receipt: the pull request, the gate's result, and merged.
F23-R13/me/ shall show:
  • the signed-in login's roles;
  • per stewarded target, the sections waiting for approval, each linked to its page;
  • their own open pull requests;
  • Step down per target.
F23-R14Every steward shown on the site shall carry self-admitted or admitted by <curator>, read from the record.
F23-R15Every control that needs a session shall be added by script after GET /session answers. A page whose fetch fails shall be the read-only page as it is today (C7). No control shall be rendered into the static HTML.

6. Budgets / limits

  • A session lasts eight hours (config).
  • POST /stewards is rate limited at 5 per login per day, and POST /approvals at 60 per login per hour (config).
  • Steward pages are one per target, rendered statically. Every role-dependent element costs one GET /session per page view.

7. Security & privacy notes

  • The approval key is a new C8 secret (v3.33 §5). It lives in the service's secret store and signs two record kinds and nothing else. No gate step reads an approval, so the key cannot change a verdict.
  • The session cookie is HttpOnly, so no script on the site can read it. That closes the cross-site scripting (XSS) route raised on 2026-10-07: a page that holds a token can leak it. Cross-site request forgery is refused three ways: SameSite=Strict, an exact Origin match, and the custom header.
  • Return paths are paths only, never URLs (AC1).
  • No data beyond what exists today: the GitHub login, the display name and the optional identity link the steward types. No email is read or kept (D-32).

9. Open questions / judgment calls

IDCall made — for review
F23-Q1A pull request, not a GitHub issue (the owner's ruling of 2026-10-07). The form does on GitHub what a filled-in form would lead to. It opens the record's own pull request, because an issue would still need a curator to turn it into a record by hand.
F23-Q2A cookie on the service host, not a token in the page. The site already calls the service cross-origin for words in review (F22-Q2). A credentialed request needs the CORS credentials flag for the site's origin only.
  • Putting the service under the site's own origin (a CloudFront /api/* behaviour) would avoid CORS. It is a hostname change that two stacks and the guide would feel.
  • A token in sessionStorage is simpler, and is exactly the XSS risk the owner was shown.
For review.
F23-Q3The pseudonym is the GitHub login on a first web sign-in. The owner asked for no pseudonym screen. The display name on a steward record is separate and editable on the form.
F23-Q4A self-signed version by a steward is final (D-3 v3.33 §3). Without it, a single-steward problem could never finalise the steward's own rewrite.
F23-Q5No MCP tools for these routes. Both roles are people who sign in with GitHub, and an agent cannot.
F23-Q6The live graph has no stewards yet (no targets/*/stewards/). There is nothing to migrate, and v1 support stays for records made by opn-gate steward commit.
F23-Q7steward/v2 revised before publication. T1 wrote admitted_by as oneOf [self, login]; "self" matches the login pattern too, so every self-admitted record was refused. Found by T5's tests and T2's independently; changed to anyOf and re-pinned (f0fee5c). Nothing had left the laptop, so D-34's never-edited applies from the push.
F23-Q8Web sign-in names. The pseudonym is the GitHub login; a login that is reserved or taken becomes <login>-gh, else 409 pseudonym-taken. The owner's login is reserved, so he signs in as thisisanameforsure-gh, while GET /session reports his login and curator: true. A revoked identity cannot sign in (403 identity-revoked). GET /session reads the cookie only.
F23-Q9What the service checks before opening. POST /stewards and POST /approvals check the target, the version and its sections, and the role read unsigned from the graph (latest record per login); the gate does the signature work after. Under reviewed the service writes the first listed curator into admitted_by; the gate enforces only that it is a listed curator, since it runs before any merge (T2's Q-a). Rate limits count refused attempts.
F23-Q10Who may end a commitment (closes a hole in R9 as written). "A step-down of either version ends either" let a stranger's SSH key end a site-made commitment, since nothing ties an SSH key to a login. An SSH step-down now ends only a commitment SSH-signed with the same key; an approval-key step-down, made by the service for the signed-in login, ends any of that login's. Admission rules apply to commitments only. Merged records are not re-checked after a key rotation.
F23-Q11targets-index/v8. Not in the task list: v7 refused a null link, so the first self-admitted steward without one would have stopped every product. v8 adds admitted_by (null for v1) and via and lets link be null; goldens, the api's golden copies (F05-Q5 recipe) and the site's accepted set moved with it. The re-pin must carry it.
F23-Q12The site's calls. "Needs a steward" counts problems that can take one and have none (open or resolved, never tutorial or calibration); the old filter keyed on no-steward, which appears only while the rule is enforced. A failed session fetch leaves the header empty (R15). Edit supersedes a whole version; Approve is per section; the licence is never defaulted. The preview inserts the service's own escaping renderer's HTML. /me/ lists open pull requests from the public /submissions.json filtered by pseudonym, since /submissions/mine takes a bearer token only.
