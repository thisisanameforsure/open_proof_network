F15 — Stewards, digestion and mathematicians' own problems

An open problem is claimable only while a named mathematician has committed to understand and write up whatever the network produces on it; whoever signs a statement's fidelity takes no proof credit on that target; an explainer can be signed as a comprehension claim and a resolved target shows how far it has been digested; mathematicians propose their own problems through an issue form; the site speaks to a sceptical mathematician; and none of the claimability enforcement goes live until fresh agents have carried three known results through the pipeline unattended.

1. Summary

On 2026-09-11 twenty-five Fields medallists condemned "the mass production of 'true/false' statements" on open problems, and the research note docs/research_math_and_ai_2026-09.html found that the network's mission as written (Erdős problems chosen for a "demonstrated resolution regime", "spare subscription credits tonight") is the condemned activity, while its design already answers most of the Leiden recommendations. The founder's four-round grill of 2026-09-15 (engineering/session-notes/2026-09-15-stewards-and-digestion.md) settled the answer, and engineering/session-notes/2026-09-15-f15-decisions-amendment.md drafts it as decisions v3.17. This spec builds that record. A steward is the D-32 writer appointed at listing rather than at resolution: a real-identity mathematician who signs a commitment to best-efforts digestion and write-up of the problem, is named on the target page, holds the write-up role jointly with every other steward, is mentioned on the graph whenever anything merges on the target, and has no power to pause or reserve anything. Under the rule, an open-problem target with no active steward is published, on the frontier and open to fidelity review, but refuses claims (D-6 v3.17); on-ramp and calibration targets are exempt. Because a prover gains from a statement slightly weaker than the conjecture, whoever signs a root's fidelity takes no proof credit on the target, and a steward chooses to sign or to prove (D-9, D-21 v3.17). An explainer may carry a signature affirming one sentence, I can explain this proof without the tool that produced it; only signed explainers count toward a resolved target's digestion state, undigested, explained or written-up, which the site shows beside resolved and the D-10 report-back carries (D-3, D-33 v3.17). A mathematician proposes a problem informally through an issue form on the graph repository; formalizing it is open work; the proposer is steward by default; intake stays curated (D-6 v3.17). The home and Docs pages are rewritten for a mathematician who has just read the declaration, with dated external evidence only and a Leiden compliance table. Last, and first in time: the steward rule is enforced only after a calibration run in which fresh agents, with no help from the founder or the build, carry three solved Erdős problems from the literature through the pipeline, at least one to resolved, every failure a typed record (Stages v3.17). Until then the 23 claimable targets stay claimable, an exposure the founder accepted. The switch is one visible file in the graph, never a quiet relaxation (D-32 v3.17 "Overturns if"). The code follows the decisions doc once v3.17 is applied (C10); until then PROTOCOL_VERSION stays 3.16 (Q1). Cites: D-3, D-4, D-6, D-9, D-10, D-13, D-19, D-21, D-22, D-27, D-32, D-33, D-34, D-35, D-36, Stages.

2. User stories

  • As a mathematician who has read the declaration, I want the home page to say what goes wrong with AI-only proofs and what this network does about it, before it asks me for anything, so that I can decide in a minute whether to read on.
  • As a mathematician, I want to put my own open problem forward in ordinary mathematics, with references, without writing Lean, and to be the person who receives whatever comes of it.
  • As a steward, I want to know exactly what I have committed to and what I get, to be named on the problem's page, and to hear on GitHub when anything merges on it, without giving the network an email address or a deadline.
  • As a prover, I want a target page to say whether the problem can be claimed and, if not, that it is waiting for a steward rather than for me, so that I do not spend compute on a problem nobody will read.
  • As a reader of a resolved target, I want to see whether anyone has understood the proof: which nodes carry a signed explainer, who vouched for them, and whether a paper exists, so that "resolved" never reads as "solved" before a human can explain it.
  • As the founder, I want the steward rule to switch on only after an unattended run on known results has shown the pipeline works, and to be able to show that run to the first mathematician I approach.

3. Scope

In: the steward record, its signature and its gate mode; the graph policy file that enforces the steward rule; the no-steward claimability reason; the signer-takes-no-proof-credit exclusion in the ledger and at attestation; signed explainers; the write-up record and the derived digestion state; the next targets-index and target versions; the merge mention; the problem proposal form and its intake path; the site's target, node, home and Docs pages, including the new copy and the Leiden table; both copies of the guide; the calibration pool, the three calibration targets, the unattended run and its record; the re-pin and the policy switch after the run passes.

Non-goals:

  • A4's adoption list and the generated-vs-explained wording of the contribution statement; blueprint rendering and leanblueprint export; reuse counts; the unused-hypothesis report; the prior-art search as a graph artifact. None has a consumer until a target closes and is written up (design record, "Deferred").
  • Any reservation or pause mechanism, for stewards or for outsiders (design record, item 4).
  • A private problem. A proposal is public from the moment it is listed; a proposer may only ask that the statement not be upstreamed (D-10 fail-open), never that it be hidden.
  • A real-identity registry beyond what exists. At Stage 0 the real-identity contributors are the graph's curators and each target's stewards (Q4); widening that set is a later feature.
  • Merge-on-green for any mode. F07-Q16 stands; the calibration run defines the owner's merge act as the protocol's, not as intervention (Q6).
  • An MCP tool for stewards or proposals. Stewards are people; their records go by pull request and their proposals by issue, both on the graph repository, and the read side is targets/index.json as today.
  • The Stage 0 Zulip post itself. It is drafted with the founder after the calibration run passes (design record, order of work, item 4) and is not a spec task.

F15-R1The system shall keep a target's steward records as targets/<id>/stewards/<n>.yaml (steward/v1), append-only: the target id; an action of commit or step-down; the steward's GitHub login (login grammar), display name and one identity link (an institutional page or ORCID record); the commitment sentence verbatim; the date; the steward's SSH public key; and an SSHSIG signature over the record's canonical body. A target's active stewards shall be the logins whose latest record is a commit. The command opn-gate steward commit|step-down shall write and sign a record with the contributor's own key through the Signer seam, and opn-gate steward check <record> shall report, for a curator, whether the signature verifies and whether the key is one the login publishes at github.com/<login>.keys (network; never run by the gate).
F15-R3The system shall read a graph-root policy.json (policy/v1) carrying steward_rule: {enforced, since, evidence}; an absent file shall mean not enforced; the file shall have the path role policy, changeable only in curator mode; and its state shall be published in the targets index and shown on the site (Q2).

F15-R5The system shall pay no proof-line ledger entry on a target to an identity holding a counting fidelity signature (a non-mechanical grade, F11-R3) on any of the target's subjects, returning the reason as curator_bar does; and opn-gate fidelity shall refuse a signing-grade certificate from an attestor who holds an active proof-line entry on that target. F15-R6The system shall keep a target's write-up records as targets/<id>/writeup/<n>.yaml (writeup/v1): kind paper or note, title, URL, date, the signer, their key and SSHSIG signature; a record shall be refused unless its signer is an active steward of the target or a listed curator. targets/<id>/note.md stays the note's text.
F15-R7The system shall derive a resolved target's digestion state as written-up when a paper write-up record exists, else explained when every proved node in the closing artifact's dependency closure carries at least one explainer with a valid signature (R8), else undigested; a target in any other status shall carry null. The derivation shall also yield, per target, the closure size, the count of closure nodes explained, and the counts of proved nodes and of proved nodes with a signed explainer, for the home page.
F15-R8The system shall keep explainer signatures as nodes/<id>/explainer/signed/<hash>-<n>.yaml (explainer-signature/v1): node, explainer hash, the affirmation sentence I can explain this proof without the tool that produced it verbatim, signer, date, key and SSHSIG signature. The explainer mode shall accept signature files alone or with new explainers, refusing by name a signature whose explainer file is absent, whose sentence differs, whose signature fails, or whose signer is not a real-identity contributor (Stage 0: an active steward of the target or a listed curator, Q4). A signature shall claim nothing about the mathematics, change no verdict and earn nothing at merge; the command opn-gate explainer sign shall write one.
F15-R9The system shall publish per target in targets-index/v6: the active stewards (login, name, link, since), the digestion state with its counts, no-steward among the possible not-claimable reasons, and the policy state once at the top; and every consumer of the version string (the site's accepted set, the api's fixture, the MCP field filter, the deploy package check, the smokes) shall be moved in the same commit.
F15-R10The site shall show: on each target page its stewards by name and link with the date each committed, or, when it has none, one cue saying what a steward commits to and receives with a link to the Docs section; for a resolved target the lead "resolved — undigested / explained / written-up" and the report-back sentence ("kernel-checked, not yet explained" or its counterpart) that D-10 carries; the no-steward reason in words when it applies; a calibration target labelled as such; on the Targets row the same steward and digestion facts; on each node page, above the unverified label, "explained and vouched for by name, date" for each valid signature; and on the home page the counts table led by "explained: n of m proved nodes".
F15-R11The site's home page shall lead, before any count, with copy for a sceptical mathematician: what goes wrong with AI-only proofs, what this network does about some of it, and what AI brings when humans stay involved; its evidence shall be dated external examples only, with no network number in the lead until the calibration run passes; it shall state what the network refuses to do (no announcements, no leaderboard, failures published). The Docs page shall carry a steward section (the commitment, what is received, what is not, how to step down), the link to the proposal form on the graph repository (from configuration, never a hostname in a template), a Leiden Declaration paragraph with a recommendation-by- recommendation compliance table drawn from research §5, and one honest sentence that the network has no answer for students. Outbound links in this copy shall pass F04-R13's checker through a documented allowlist, never by loosening the rule.
F15-R12When a merge lands on a target with active stewards, the post-merge job shall mention each steward's login in the bot commit message (gate: #N pass · cc @login …), the logins taken from the merged tree's records and validated against the login grammar; the message helper shall take the stewards and the workflow change shall be inert on the old pin. The notification's arrival shall be verified live before the rule is relied on, with a pull-request comment as the priced fallback (Q5).
F15-R13The graph repository shall carry a problem proposal issue form, .github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/problem-proposal.yml, asking for the statement in ordinary mathematics, references, why it is believed open, the area, whether the proposer will steward it, whether to skip upstreaming and why, and a checkbox acknowledging that listing publishes the problem; target/v2 shall add source.kind: proposal (ref: the issue URL), proposer, calibration (default false) and upstream_opt_out; intake new shall refuse a proposal whose ref is not an issue on the graph repository, and calibration: true on the open track. The proposer's steward record, signed offline, shall travel in the intake pull request when they accept (Q8); without one the target is listed and, under the rule, refuses claims. Formalizing a proposal's statement is open work done on the issue, not in the graph.
F15-R14The calibration run: (a) the pool shall be every problem in the committed seed dataset with a Lean statement in the pinned registry whose erdosproblems.com status is proved, solved or disproved without a Lean proof, computed by a committed query (29 rows at the 2026-09 dataset); (b) three shall be chosen from it, graded easy, medium and one needing a skeleton, with the reasons recorded; (c) each shall be taken in on the formalization track with calibration: true, the literature proof cited as prior art, through the wave driver; (d) fresh agents given only the site URL shall carry them with no help on mathematics, tooling or Lean from the founder or the build, the founder acting only as the merge actor on a green check (Q6), every other act logged and failing the run; (e) the run passes when all three go claim → precheck → submit → merge, at least one target reaches resolved, and every failure leaves a typed D-13 record; (f) the record is engineering/evidence/F15/calibration.md; (g) only after a pass shall the policy switch pull request be opened.
F15-R15Both copies of AGENTS.md and the human-funnel document shall describe steward records, explainer signatures, write-up records, proposals and calibration targets, executed by the guide's runner where a command is shown; amendment notes shall be added to F03 (products), F07 (explainer mode), F11-R4/F14-R1 (claimability) and F14 (target/v2); PROTOCOL_VERSION shall become 3.17 in the first code commit after the decisions doc carries v3.17.
F15-R16The system shall prove the path in the fast tier: a proposal-shaped target taken in with a signed steward record is claimable under the enforced rule and unclaimable after the steward steps down, and claimable either way with the rule off; a fidelity signer's proof pays nothing and a prover's signature is refused; a resolved target moves undigested → explained → written-up as signatures and the write-up record land; the merge message names the stewards; the products reproduce byte for byte; and the rendered site shows each state.

6. Budgets / limits

  • The fast tier grows by no more than 10 s; test_lifecycle_f15.py under 10 s. The tier already runs about 98 s against conventions §2's minute, a debt this spec does not add to beyond that.
  • Calibration: three targets, one pull request each at intake; the run is bounded by the agents' own budgets, recorded per target; the founder's merge acts are the only pacing.
  • The Mathlib image belongs to CI; the calibration targets import Mathlib, so any docker-tier admission test runs there (F11-Q17).
  • Stewards per target: any number; one is enough. No cap.

7. Security & privacy notes

  • Steward, write-up and explainer-signature records are evidentiary (C9): they enter the graph only through a merge the gate checked, append-only, and are verified offline through the Signer seam (ssh-keygen in the gate). The api verifies none of them.
  • No email is stored for a steward; the mention is a login in a commit message. A login is validated against GitHub's grammar before it enters any message or page, so a record cannot inject text there.
  • A steward's identity link is contributor-supplied free text: rendered as a link only after the record validates, through the same allowlist mechanism F11-Q11 built for target sources; the site never invents an outbound link.
  • The proposal form's text lives in GitHub issues, never in the graph; only a curator's intake moves any of it across the boundary, quoted under the existing quotation policy.
  • policy.json is curator-mode only, so the steward rule cannot be switched by a submission. Switching it either way is a visible diff on the record repository.
  • No new secret or permission unless Q5's fallback is taken, in which case the post-merge job (a push-triggered job that runs no contributor code before the step) would gain pull-requests: write, an owner's call recorded in C8 if made.

F15-Q1The doc leads. Decisions v3.17 is drafted in engineering/session-notes/2026-09-15-f15-decisions-amendment.md and not applied (the doc is v3.16 on 2026-09-16). T2 onward is built only once it is; T1, the site copy draft (T11) and the calibration pool research (T14a) can proceed now. F15-Q2The switch is a file, not a re-pin. The design record said the rule goes live "with a re-pin"; but any re-pin during the calibration run (a bug fix, say) would then flip 23 targets unclaimable. A graph-root policy.json, absent until the run passes and flipped by one curator pull request naming the evidence, is a visible diff that honours D-32 v3.17's "never quietly relaxed" in both directions. Proposed addition to the v3.17 text: D-35's list of what the graph holds gains policy.json and .github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/, both curator-owned.
F15-Q3The signer's proof bar covers the whole target, not only the root, because every interior node is proved in service of the root (amendment gap 2). It bars the proof line only; attempts, statements and write-ups pay as for a curator.
F15-Q4Who is real-identity at Stage 0. D-22 says reviewers, adjudicators and writers are real-identity, and nothing in the graph records who is. For explainer signatures and write-up records the set is the target's active stewards plus the listed curators, both curator-checked; anyone else's signature is refused by name rather than recorded as a second class. Widening the set is one function when a registry exists.
F15-Q5The mention channel. GitHub's notification docs list "your username @mentioned" as a trigger without saying whether a commit message counts, and the bot commit is the one place the post-merge job already writes with no new permission. So the message carries the mention, and T15 verifies the notification arrives live (the F06 pattern: probe, then rely). Fallback: a comment on the merged pull request, which needs pull-requests: write on the post-merge job (§7).
F15-Q6What "no intervention" means. Nothing merges on green (F07-Q16), so a person must press merge. The run counts a merge on a green check as the protocol's own act and every other act by the founder or the build (a hint, a fix, a re-run, a witness) as an intervention that fails the run. The alternative is to build merge-on-green first; the founder's call.
F15-Q7How identity is checked. The steward's record carries the link and the key; steward check tells the curator whether the key is one the login publishes; the curator reads the link; the merge is the check, recorded by the merge itself. No second signature.
F15-Q8The signature binds the record, not the pull request's author, so a curator may carry a steward's signed record in an intake pull request. A steward with a GitHub account may equally open their own.
F15-Q9Calibration targets. They sit on the formalization track (D-6's second-class row, which the amendment widens to "results already known"), flagged calibration: true so the site labels them and no count of open-problem work includes them. They are never relabelled known-result, which is a closed status; they are known from day one.
F15-Q10Numbers on the home page. The design record says no network numbers until the run passes; D-36 says the home page shows live counts. The lead paragraph carries none; the counts table stays below it, led by explanation coverage, because a count the site already publishes is not an announcement.
F15-Q11The closing artifact for the closure is the root's proof, or the resolves variant's where the target resolved by variant (D-30); a partial variant closes nothing and starts no digestion state.
F15-Q12The calibration pool query is committed beside the seed dataset and read by a test, so the number in R14 is a fact the suite checks, not a sentence that rots. The three chosen targets and their grades are recorded in T14a's evidence before any intake.
